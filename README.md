# infinitelight
a discovery based visual art experience

## Play
Served as a static site (GitHub Pages: Settings → Pages → Deploy from branch `main`, folder `/`).
`index.html` opens the title screen.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | Redirects to the title screen |
| `TitleSplash.html` | Title screen: Continue / Start (New map) / Guide (discovered first), under the ring round Infinite (`placeMenu`); the title floats on water: a click ripples it, a double click splashes, a drag leaves a wake, the cursor's stain is laid in it, and the rings bend the title as they pass (an SVG displacement map made from them, `#tw`, only while they last); its text can never be selected |
| `Surface_Map_HUB.html` | The Surface Map: a 3D scene (the hex grid drawn as its ground, a tilting, zooming camera, your ducks swimming round it), day clock, map save, Esc menu |
| `chromatic_nebula.html` | The surface (one level). Runs on its own, or inside the map / title screen with `?hub=1` |
| `join.html` | Play together: a friend's surface, shown as it plays there; your pointer shines and collects on it |
| `content/` | **The game's content, as CSV files you can edit** (and `levels.json`, the levels and prefabs exported from Build mode): tuners (every tuned value and the Tune menu), discoveries, shops and their items and prices, fortunes and wonders, events, Guide entries, ducks, letters, and every other piece of text (`strings.csv`). Every page reads them as it loads; see `content/README.md`. In the text, `{name}` is a value filled in as it shows: `strings.csv`'s last column (`values`) says what each of a row's names is (`name = $0 — what it is`, $0 / $1… being what the code hands it); its `value.<name>` rows at the end are values any text can use (an expression over the tuners) |
| `content.js` | Reads `content/*.csv` (every page loads it first): `csvRead`, and `txt(key, …)` for the text in `strings.csv`; and the loading screen (`loading(why, on)`): black, ∞ with a glowing bead running round it, `load.text` under it with a glow passing over it now and then. It covers the map until it is drawn (the surface's word, every hex's picture, the shops), entering a surface until it is drawn, coming back to the map, the title on the site's first load, and leaving a page. It is one screen through every step: a page opened from one that was showing it has it up at once (`il_load` in sessionStorage), and from its very first paint shows nothing but it (a line at the top of its `<head>` marks it `il-arrive` before anything is drawn: only the loading screen is visible until it lifts), the bead and glow keep time with the clock so a new page never starts them over, and one step straight after another keeps it up rather than fading it out and in |
| `shared.js` | What the surface and the Surface Map draw the same way — the Dark Remnant's glow, the bow wake, and the rubber duck: its baked meshes and markings (`DUCK_MESH`) and its WebGL renderer (onto a 2D canvas, or straight into a 3D scene) |
| `shops/` | Shop signs (`sign_<shop>`, SVG). The buildings are 3D models (`SHOP_BLD` / `shopModel` in `shared.js`): walls with window and door openings, a 3D shop inside (shelves of goods, a counter along the side away from the door with its front in the shop's colour, drapes in the shop's colour in every window, a lamp), doors that open while the shop's menu is open, glass in every window (drawn as glass: it reflects the map round it, faintly while the lights are on), a neon OPEN sign in a ground floor window while open (`shop.open.sign`), frames, roof, sign scaffolding and the sign mounted on it; lit by the time of day. The shops themselves, their hours and what they sell are `content/shops.csv` and `content/shop_items.csv` |
| `tools/build_duck_meshes.js` | Bakes the two 3D duck meshes (classic, Big Head) and the duck markings texture into `shared.js`, from `tools/Duck_Sheet.svg` (vector views: crisp markings, a lighter mesh, lines and eyes shaded by bump) or, with `DUCK_STRIP=tools/duck_strip.png`, the old pixel strip. First `cd tools && npm install` (SVG rendering), then `node tools/build_duck_meshes.js` after changing the drawing or the pipeline |

**New nebula versions must keep the name `chromatic_nebula.html`** — the map and title screen load it by that name. When you replace it, bump the `&v=` number on its `<iframe>` in `Surface_Map_HUB.html` and `TitleSplash.html` so browsers fetch the new copy instead of a cached one.

## Players
- Several players can be saved in one browser, each with their own save (map, items, ducks, letters, souvenirs, name, buoy). Player 1's keys are as they always were; player n's are `p<n>:`-prefixed, in localStorage and the IndexedDB session (`content.js` does it for every page, so nothing else needs to know). The setup (tuners, panels) is shared.
- The title screen: **Continue** asks which player when more than one has a map saved (name and day); **New player** starts another (their name is asked on the map). The player chosen is kept for the tab (or `?profile=n`).

## The cursor
- One cursor everywhere (`gameCursor` in `content.js`): the system cursor never shows, in any state, menu or card (the title screen, the map's menus and shops, the surface's Guide, cards and panels). Over the surface's scene and dock the surface draws its own; everywhere else the same ring is drawn over everything. Its click reaction is the surface's on every page: a press shrinks it in, held it flares past rest, a release eases it back, a right-click punches it out (`cursorClick*`).
- On the map, a click leaves a little ripple, a double click a splash, and a click-swipe (a drag) ripples and a wake behind it. A click that does nothing to a hex flashes it like tapped glass (with its tap), and the feed flashes with why.
- Its trail on the ground (`cursorStain`) is lit in the colour of the light at that time of day (`DUCK_SUN`: warm at dawn and dusk, white at midday, blue at night), not always white.
- The trail and its stain (`cursorTrailRecord`, `cursorStain`) live in `content.js` with the cursor, so every page has them: a surface (under its particles), the Surface Map's ground and the title's water. Its colour is the light's hue at full strength (never dimmer at night).
- A menu button's bracketed context ("Quit to main menu (saves this surface)") is not on the button: pointing at it says it in the feed (`parenHints`, each page's `ilHint`).

## The feed (the Surface Map)
- One place for every message: nothing is said mid-screen and no card comes up over the map; something seen for the first time on the map is said in the feed (and discovered: its card is in the Guide).
- Put away (its ▾), the feed still pops up for a message or an alert, then goes; its ▴ always shows; pointing into its place below the items brings it back up while you are there.
- Status & collections is only as wide as its title, buttons and tabs, with a margin; its pages fit that.

## Parades (the Surface Map's lake)
- When you have four or more ducks of one kind (a Colour, Size or Material; nine at most), they come out in a parade, once a day for each kind: one of each and the classic one, in a line like ducklings behind their mother, all the way round the spill just off its shore. Size goes biggest first (in a full one, the Big Head last, behind the smallest); otherwise the special one leads (Rainbow, Gold), the other specials next, then the rest. While it passes no other duck or bottle comes in. A touch spins one and a click dunks it, but none of them ever leaves the line. Pointing at one says whose parade it is and who is in it.
- **Parade Day** (a dim fortune): no other duck or bottle comes; your own ducks (as many as you have, nine at most) parade on the hexes themselves, two to a hex, winding ring by ring through every hex, one hex further each time of day. Nothing crosses a hex they are in, as with a Rift.
- Lake ducks and bottles: a fast brush pushes one off the way it was hit (and only that); a slow one spins it where it is. When its time is up it heads straight off the screen (if it is still in view long after, it sinks away): nothing vanishes in view. They bump into each other like points on a surface, pushed apart and turned.

## Playing together (the Surface Map)
- **Menu → Play together** opens a room (PeerJS; `?net=bc` plays across two tabs of one browser); its link (`Surface_Map_HUB.html?join=…`) is in the menu and copied. Up to three friends join, any time, each with their own name, buoy and items: their own save, so what they had is theirs. The host's map is the one played; a friend joins on a free hex beside the source, and everyone's buoy floats on their hex.
- **Turns** go round each time of day in a snake (1 2 3 4, then 4 3 2 1; the bar under the time shows the order, whose turn in bold). On your turn: use as many items as you like, enter your hex's surface, then end your turn by **moving** or **Rest**. When everyone has had theirs, the time of day moves on. Off your turn you can look round from your own view (and play with your lake).
- **Surfaces**: whoever enters plays it in their own game (their items, their rules), and everyone else watches it (its frames, through the host). A player on the **same hex** shines and collects in it with them, with their own heat, and what they collect is theirs (it goes to their own save); a player elsewhere can only watch. A friend's surface cleared or torn is played on the host's map.
- **Shared luck**: any player's fortune or Daily Wonder that changes the map or what drops counts for everyone (`MP_RUN`); ones that only change your own items (three at a time, an Endless item) stay yours. Anything one player discovers (Guide entries, events, a Card of Secrets' reveal) is discovered by everyone who has not yet.
- **Items, from anywhere**: watching someone's surface you can use your own items on it (Depth, Balance, Magnetic, Neutron, Pulsing, Spindle: pick one under the picture, then click), on whichever hex you are; it costs you, field upkeep included.
- **Lakes, bottles and letters** are each player's own: a bottle shines for you only when what is in it is new to you, and opening one takes nothing from anyone else's.
- The host plays a friend's actions on its map (`mpAct`: from their hex, with their items; the cost comes off their own); the friend's own map is left untouched while they play.

## Your buoy, Status & collections
- **Your buoy** (`buoyModel` in `shared.js`, drawn on the map by `buoyDraw`): a float in your colour (`BUOY_COLS`: the six effect colours, one between each, white, greys and black) half under the surface of the hex you stand on, reflected in it. It bobs, rocks (`mapBuoySway`), turns slowly, ripples the water, and turns and rocks a little more when the cursor touches it. Your name is on a framed 3D sign on its mast, lit from within, and a can light sits on top in a yoke, turned to point where its light goes (`buoyCanModel`): its beam (`buoyBeam`, a faint cone drawn in 3D from the can) lights a spot on the surface itself, in the ground's own shader (no glow, no edge line): colours there brighten while black stays black, white takes a white sheen, shops in it are lit by it as a real light in their 3D drawing (`shopLight`: from where the lamp is, as strong as the spot is there; on their walls as diffuse light and a sheen, glinting on their glass; `T.mapShopLight`), and remnants and rifts are left as they are. The cursor lights the ground under it the same way, softer. Shops are reflected in the ground under them (`shopReflect`): each from its own drawing seen from below and turned over (so the reflection turns with the building), all drawn solid into one layer, nearer over farther (never see-through to each other), laid on the ground once with a `lighten` blend, a little brighter than the buildings so a closed one still shows: it shows only where the ground is dark (the darkness seeping through the bright; `mapShopReflect`, 0.7). By day a shop's sign, lamp and lit room are dimmer (about half at midday, full in the evening) and its glass shows the street more, stretched away from it as light at a slant is, circling the hexes next to it clockwise (`mapSpotMs`) and swinging onto the hex you point at when you could move there or use the item in hand on it. A move sails it over with a wake; a String fires it there like a Point. Entering a surface, it is dunked the whole way while the camera comes over the hex, then turns to look straight down with the hex filling the frame, then goes down into it, and the surface fades in (`mapEnterMs`; the clean loading screen only if the surface is not drawn yet); coming back out is the same, backwards. Playing together, everyone's view does it with the player going in. The can light points straight up while it sinks or rises (and goes out under), and on a quick dip when you click it for Status. It sinks to sleep when you Rest at Midnight and bobs up in the morning. A move to a hex you can see leaves the camera where you put it; one out of view (and a scene change: a new day, a Rift's throw, right-click) brings it to the buoy, unless you take the view (drag or zoom) on the way; a scene's own camera move cannot be taken. Every scripted camera move is slow and smooth: following your buoy, framing it for Status and back to your hex ease through one critically damped spring (`camSpring`, `mapCamEase` 2.4 s: it starts gently and never overshoots, the same at any frame rate); a scene's own moves (the day's end, back from Status) are eased with no jolt at either end and take `mapCamSlow` (1.8×) longer; the dive into a surface (`mapEnterMs`, 3 s) runs its three parts overlapping, so it never stops between them, its zoom even in scale. Your own drags and zooms are direct. Status opens on the right with the map still showing to its left: the camera puts your buoy in the middle of that space and zooms in on it as it rises (`statusCam`), and goes back to your own view when it closes. The spot light swings at one steady rate the short way round (`mapSpotTurn`), and its slow cycle round the hexes next to you carries on from wherever it is. A Rift torn under a buoy throws it to the innermost free hex (nearest first), else back to main street. Name and colour are asked on a new game, and can be changed in Status & collections → **Buoy**. The latest of each souvenir you have kept hangs on its rim. `buoys` holds every buoy on the map, yours first.
- **Status & collections** (click your buoy, or its slot in the map's dock or a surface's inventory): the same menu as the Guide, with its own tabs: **Status**, **Buoy** (its name and colour), **Ducks** (one of each type you have, at true size, floating on a pond or adrift in space: a switch toggles them, and Invert turns either white), **Letters** (the book of letters from the bottles) and **Souvenirs**.

## Build mode
- On the title screen, **~** shows **Build mode** (`chromatic_nebula.html?build=1`): a surface for making and testing scenes and prefabs. Nothing in it is saved to your game (items, ducks, discoveries).
- The surface is never cleared there, so Dark Remnants can be placed: one that forms (or is put down with Create) waits where it is, drawn, until **Test**. Remove takes it away again. Rifts don't form.
- Every item is there and endless (∞), everything is revealed, and the item cache shows with only its on / off. Create has a **Surface** type: click to turn the surface dark or bright (white).
- The surface's own panels are all there, as on any surface (Rebuild, Modes, Tune with every tuner, Image, the stats and the log; ~ hides them), with a **Build ▾** button first at the top left that opens Build mode's menu under it: a level's **name**, **Save** (kept in this browser, IndexedDB `build`), **Load…**, **New surface**, **Blank**, **▶ Test** (the surface plays as it would in a game: its Dark Remnant starts, a Rift can form, it can be cleared; **◼ Back to build** puts it back as it was), **Delete**, **Export** (downloads `levels.json`, every level and prefab: put it in `content/`, where the game reads it as it loads) and **Title**.
- A level keeps everything on the surface (its nebula packed small: one particle in full, the rest only what differs), its Dark Remnants and its ducks, which are collectable when it is played.
- The create tool and the item cache are no longer in a game's ~ panels: they are Build mode's.
- **Prefabs** (Create → Prefab): **Select** parts (click, or drag a box, as the Inertializer: points, remnants, pigments and shapes, with their motion), then in the bar's PREFAB row give it a name, the times of day it can appear (from – to, the map's clock; across Midnight is fine) and how it phases (in, out and stay ms; stay 0 = it stays) and **Save prefab** (kept with the levels; Export writes them to `levels.json` too). The **Phaser** sets the phasing for the whole prefab (click anywhere) or for one part (click it: its own in / out / stay, orange). **Place** phases one in where you click.
- **Phasing**: a part grows from a point, all black, then a white flash runs out from it and reveals it, real: until then it passes over everything; once real it is fully active, and moving at once if it was. Phasing out is the same, backwards.
- **By time of day**: when a surface is entered at a time of day (a map hex: the map's clock), by `prefabChance` one prefab whose times include that hour appears: a still one phases in somewhere on the surface (anywhere: it wraps), one that moves may instead drift in from off an edge. Its own phasing applies, so one with a stay phases out again. The prefabs are the content files' (`levels.json`); in Build mode, the ones made there too, and **Test at** a time of day tries it (the feed says which appeared).
- **At an edge** (Place, Move or Duplicate within 24 px of one), it is pushed fully off the screen (`prefabEdgeBuf` px more) and waits there, shown half see-through with a yellow border and an arrow, where it will come on. When the surface is played (or tested), it drifts in, each part coming on as it crosses the edge. If its motion would never bring it on screen (still, or moving away), it is not placed, and the feed says why.

## Rifts
- **On a surface** (`riftCheck`, Rift mass in one area): a glass crack runs across the screen at a random angle through that mass, glowing with the Dark Remnant's pulse (`riftCrackMs`), then snaps open, faster than its wave (`riftOpenMs`), into a pure black stripe (`riftWMin`–`riftWMax` px wide) with long edges and nearly flat bends; whatever it opens over is lost. Its surface's clear says it was a Rift. A wave then runs out from it on both sides, parallel to it, and everything is drawn straight across to it and consumed, with the Dark Remnant's bursts where it hits. It is the Dark Remnant's own collapse (`blackHole` with `.rift`: every distance is to its line, `bhVec`), so its settings govern the rest; remnants can still be collected at the last second. Never a duck.
- **On the Surface Map** (`riftCell`): the hex is gone, a scar (tapered to points at its ends, like a brush stroke) in the Dark Remnant's pulsing glow, opening as the map comes back: it snaps open, a ring of a wave goes out as a nova's does seen from above, and everything round it is drawn in (a bright clear it takes shatters into glowing white, a shop on it is torn to pieces, `riftFx`); and the hexes beside it are drained (their particles lost, nothing shared out to them or into it). A drained hex can be stood on and moved off, but not entered or cleared. Moves cannot cross a Rift, and a String cannot shoot through one (a straight line only: hexes behind one are not lit as targets). Scars side by side line up into one edge; three that all touch merge into a triangle (`riftsDraw`). They are gone at Midnight (the new map).

## The Guide
Every entry is a card in one format (name, a live demo, page and Tier I–V, then its details and live math, each behind its own discovery). Pages: **Discoveries** (how to play, how to clean up, and the events witnessed), **Taxonomy** (every kind of thing and what it becomes, drawn with the game's own glyphs; its cards below), Items, Shapes, Surface Map, **Shops** (How shops work on top: main street, pop-ups, buying; then each shop's card, only its sign's shape until it has opened, with what it does and a tab for each currency it takes), Ducks, Glossary. (Status, your ducks, letters and souvenirs are in **Status & collections**, below.) A shop's first opening is an event (it is discovered then: the Corner Store and Gift Shop on the first morning, the others when they first open). Discoveries made together come as a hand: one card shown, the others below it to pick; closing it eases time back in slowly (`evResumeMs`).

## Play together
Esc on a surface → **Invite a friend** copies a link (`join.html?room=…`). The friend's page shows the host's surface as a live video (PeerJS / WebRTC, peer to peer; the PeerJS public server only makes the introduction) and sends their pointer. On the host it shines, wipes, mops and collects with the same shine cap (`maxActivePoints`) but its own heat; what the friend collects is added to the friend's own save. Add `&net=bc` to the surface's URL to play between two tabs of one browser (BroadcastChannel, no network).

## Performance
The Surface Map draws its ground on a 2D canvas and sends it to the GPU every frame, so its size is the main cost: `mapPlanePx` caps it, and its sharpness follows the zoom. While the map's window is not the one in use it draws `mapIdleFps` (8) frames a second. A hex's picture is built straight from the surface's particle map (only the cells it shows, its blur passes drawn into the picture, no full frame and no start snapshot): about 12 ms instead of 60, one a frame, so a whole map of new pictures eases in over a second or two. A hex's coverage is its value itself, to 1% (`cellCov`: a value of 0.3 is a surface 30% covered, out to full at `mapCovFull`), so a picture is rebuilt whenever a hex's value moves by 1%; however bare, its particles keep their full mass and energy. The light moves round to a new time of day over `mapSunMs` (4.5 s). The performance log keeps only the last 600 frames, 25 slowest and 400 events, so a map left open does not grow it.

## Keys
- **Esc** — pause menu (sound + volume, readouts, Guide, quit). On the map: sound, Guide, hex-layer tuners, quit.
- **Purchases** on the map show what they did everywhere at once: the feed sums it up (what was spent and what came), each purse and item it changed counts it off and glows (green came, red spent), the time of day lights up when the Clock smith turns it, the row bought flashes, and the Status rows that changed light up
- **Typing a tuner's value**: on a surface, in Build mode and on the Surface Map alike, the value beside each slider is a box: type a number, then Enter (or click away) sets it, corrected into its range and onto its nearest step (`tuneSnap`, `tuneValInput` in `shared.js`); Esc puts it back.
- **Tune** on the map: the Surface Map's own tuners are on their own tab (the map and camera, the day, hex pictures, lake and bottles, shops, pop-up ranks, dock, performance); the Esc menus and the title screen's menu are drawn as a shop's list
- **~** (or H) — hide / show the panels, on a surface and on the Surface Map
- **Surface Map**: Modes (Telemetry: each hex's value, coverage and ring on the ground; Stats: fps, frame times and the hex grid; the Performance log) and Tune (every tuner, as on a surface). Lake ducks (and bottles): brush one slowly and it spins, hit it fast and it putters off, click it and it is dunked; how fast it spins, how hard it is pushed and how much it turns as it goes all follow how fast the cursor moved. Now and then a message in a bottle floats in (`mapBottleMs`): a 3D clear, green or brown glass bottle (drawn by the duck renderer, `bottleModel` in `shared.js`), half under the water and reflected in it. It drifts like a duck; clicking it opens a letter (`LETTERS`), or, for one bottle in four (`mapBottleDuck`), a duck, which joins your collection (one you did not have bobs up onto the lake). A duck's bottle is gold for an exotic, opaque white for a classic, and shines when the duck is new to you. Bottles float upright, leaning, about half under; an empty one can be dunked like a duck. A brush or a hit counts once, until the cursor has come fully off; a dunk sinks it under the waterline and it bobs back up, and the empty bottle goes back on the water. Letters found are kept (`il_letters_v1`) and read in the **Letters** book (Status & collections) **Double dab** a duck (or an opened bottle) and it goes under, then pops up well out of the water for a moment. **Drag or wipe** over one and it is pushed under and carried with the cursor (the map does not pan meanwhile); let go and it comes up there, on the water or on the spill, but never under a shop (`shopUnder`: its footprint; it slides out from under first). On the spill it wades off, outwards and round any shop or Rift in its way (`duckBlocked`); with no way off for `mapDuckTrapMs` (6 s) it sinks under and is gone.
- **Test mode** (in Modes, on the map and on a surface; `TEST` in `shared.js`, each player's own): turning it on keeps a copy of your save (the surface's purse, discoveries, ducks and run: `il_test_snap_inv`; the map: `il_test_snap_map`), then turns on **Free purchases** (every shop price 0, ducks too, up to each purse's cap; `shopPrice`), discovers and unlocks everything (`discAll`: the interface, object types, events, the Guide and its map; `unlockAll`: every upgrade owned, so its mechanics run, and one of every duck), and fills every item (`testFill`). Turning it off puts the save and the map back exactly as they were and reloads. Its tools show only while it is on: those three again, and on a surface **Clear: bright / dark / rift** (`testClear`, through the usual clear); on the map the **Test tool** (`armed='test'`): click any hex for a menu (`testPick`) to make it a bright or dark clear, a Rift, or particles again (at the value shared out now: `testUnset`, `testHex`); on a bright hex, any pop-up at any rank there, and on the source hex one on main street, in the oldest one's place (`testShop`).
- **~** (or H) — hide / show the panels (everything but the time of day, the inventory and its feed), on a surface and on the Surface Map: one setting for both. They start hidden
- **Rest** — at Midnight you are too tired to move: a Rest button under the time of day ends the day, so a last thing can be done first (a surface, a shop, an item)
- **Surface Map dock**: pointing at an item names it and says what it does; with it in hand, the feed says what it would do on the hex under the cursor and what it costs. Every button (the ▾ too) has its own see-through backing. The **▾** hides the feed and reduces the dock: each button then stays down out of the way until you point at its own place, it is in hand, or it was just used, changed or toggled (`mapDockLingerMs`). ~ leaves the dock and the feed as they are (it hides only the ▾)
- **Surface Map**: Modes (Telemetry: each hex's value, coverage and ring on the ground; Stats: fps, frame times and the hex grid; the Performance log) and Tune (every tuner, as on a surface). Lake ducks (and bottles): brush one slowly and it spins, hit it fast and it putters off, click it and it is dunked; how fast it spins, how hard it is pushed and how much it turns as it goes all follow how fast the cursor moved. Now and then a message in a bottle floats in (`mapBottleMs`): it drifts like a duck, and clicking it opens a letter (`LETTERS` in `shared.js`)
- **Spindle** (armed on the map): a cleared hex back to a surface, or an open hex below 1.00 wound up to 1.00 and held there until cleared. An unwound surface keeps its clear's look: a bright one starts white (its particles back), a Dark Remnant one dark
- **Merges** on the map are one shape: the merged hexes fill edge to edge with only their outer edges drawn
- **Camera**: held on the spill, not the source: it zooms to fit the spill with a margin of lake round it (`mapLakeMargin`) for a bottle or a duck to swim into, follows it as it grows and shrinks, and stops dead at its extent (a rectangle round the hexes' corners plus the margin; no spring back). Zoom in and it stays where you put it until you zoom back out. At a day's end it moves once, smoothly, from where it is to where the next day starts (its middle and zoom), over the collapse
- A bright clear collects everything left on the surface at once
- **Create → Duck** (on a surface): Duck in space floats one of any kind there; Duck on the pond sends it to swim on the Surface Map's lake (it bobs up when you are back on the map)
- **Space** / click your hex — enter its surface
- Map: drag to look around (the source stays in view); right-click to come back to your hex

## Shops (Surface Map)
- **Main street** (turned so the front of each in front of the source, and the back of each behind it, runs along the source hex's edge; round the source: two behind it, two in front; always there, lit only while open): Corner Store (24 hours), Clock smith (Morning – Dusk: any other time of day, 2,000 Bright ×2 for the next one, then ×3, ×5, ×8, ×13, ×21, ×34 further away; or this time yesterday — the map as it was then, you where you were — or tomorrow — a clean map a day on, before any move, you beside main street — for 250,000), XACT change (Dawn – Afternoon: Dim at 9 for each Bright, Magnetic, Neutron and Pulsing at 1 for every 10, in lots of 100, 10,000 and 1,000,000 Bright; sold back, under their own tabs, for a little less; the Wood Duck's good fortune and Short Change move what it pays, Fair and Bad Exchange both ways), Gift Shop (entering it wipes the save; souvenirs are bought from your last Bright and kept). A closed shop's glass can be tapped (it rings and rattles; five quick taps and someone inside shouts at you); with the Shining Duck's good fortune, Friendly Neighborhood, a tap opens it up off hours. A shop is only hit where it is drawn (its own pixels: `shopAt`), so one behind another can still be clicked. Tapping a closed shop's glass makes it glint; only the fifth, the yell, shakes it. A sign's black catches the light of the day.
- **Pop-ups** open on a bright clear whose surface collected enough of one thing (rank 1–3 by the `mapShop<Kind>1..3` tuners: a small store, a tall narrow one of two storeys as its concept drawing, then the wide two-storey one, the biggest). Every building is a 3D model with its room built inside, on every storey (a floor between them; shelves of goods on each, a lamp to each, the counter downstairs), warm white walls with a hint of the shop's colour (its colour is in its goods and drapes, drawn back to the sides). Every opening is filled with clear glass (`gc` glass: nearly see-through, so the room shows; what it reflects grows at a slant, with glints on top); closed, the room is dark behind it). One of each: a better rank moves the shop to the new clear and the old one is torn down. A Dark Remnant clear that earns one opens it on the nearest open bright hex or free spot in the source hex. Every turn, each cleared surface that earned a pop-up and has none open tries again (a hex a shop moved out of, a dark clear that found no room, a holiday). Every shop is drawn at main street's scale (`mapStreetSize`); the two-storey stores of ranks 2 and 3 are drawn larger (`mapShopUpScale`, 1.7) so their doors match main street's. Which opens first: the DUCK SHOP, then the best-ranked exotic one (NEUTRINOS, MAGNETS, the PULSE; a tie at random), then BRiGHT IDEAS, last Dim's.
- **Where a pop-up stands**: in the back half of its bright hex (`popSpot`, `mapPopBack`: 0.42 of a hex), so a buoy can be on that hex (or sail into it) without touching it: a buoy on a hex with a pop-up floats as far forward (`buoySpot`, everyone's).
- **Shop hours** (`content/shops.csv` open / close): both are times of day the shop is open (the first and the last: one that closes at Midnight is open on the last turn of the day). BRiGHT IDEAS and Dim's Dawn – Dusk, MAGNETS and NEUTRINOS Morning – Evening, the PULSE Afternoon – Midnight (the DUCK SHOP: always). A pop-up is lit and open only in its hours. A clear can only bring one that is open at that time of day: one it earned that is closed is **missed** (said in the feed; that hex will not try it again) and is **Coming soon** on main street straight away: it takes the source hex's spot of the oldest pop-up there (`hubPut`: first in, first out, whatever its rank; one of each: not if one already stands at that rank or better), standing closed with a lit COMING SOON sign and its name (`soonSign`, readable at any time of day), and the next day it opens there (`popSoonOpen`). Earned later the same day after all, it is no longer coming. A new pop-up waits while its hex's clear plays, then rises out of the bright (`shopRise`, `mapShopRiseMs`) and turns on and opens once it is up.
- **Dim's Carnival of Discovery** (its secrets), each once a day: a Card of Secrets (50 bright: pick one of three face-down cards; what it hides, an event or Guide entry you have not found, is discovered for good and its own card is shown; gone from the menu once nothing is left), Fortune Telling (200: a good fortune named for a duck or a dim one, and every fortune is for tomorrow: it takes hold as the next day starts, `fortuneStart`), a Daily Wonder (2,000: keep one of three bonuses with a catch, at once, through the time of day before the one it was drawn at tomorrow: drawn at Morning, it lasts to the end of Dawn the next day, `run.bonus.thru`). Status says both plainly: today's fortune, tomorrow's, and the time the wonder lasts through. The dim fortune **Dangerift**: after every clear of any kind (bright, dark or a Rift), a bright clear beside a Rift is removed and a dark one becomes Rift too, and so on. The draw and the card picked are shown by the surface over the map, in the one card format: what it changes in play set apart (monospace, ruled) from what is said of it (the flavour), and a live demo — a good fortune's own duck on the lake, a dim fortune's Dim or Dark Remnants (or the map rule it bends), a wonder's item at work.
- A shop's menu lists everything at once (a long list makes it taller): a tab for each thing it takes, by its symbol (named in the feed), every purse in the dock with the open tab's lit, the item under the cursor described in the feed, and Status at the right.
- **DUCK SHOP** also sells Luck Charms (+5% duck chance today, +1% on the next drop, ×10 the price each one, back to 5,000 each day) and the Lucky Duck (1,000,000, for good: base duck chances ×2, each miss +4%).
- Today's fortune, wonder and charms, and the Keys a fortune took, are the run's state in the surface's save (`run`); the **Status** tab of Status & collections (and the panel beside a shop's menu) shows them with the wallet, its caps and the upgrades bought.
- Duck types (`DUCK_TYPES`, surface): colours (six, then ☆ Crystalize and Eclipse, then ★ Rainbow), sizes (Microscopic, Wee, Tiny, Small, Big, Huge, Massive, Mega, ★ Big Head), materials (Wood, Silhouette (a Dark Remnant's beating glow round its outline, as it turns), Paper, Glass, Metal, Dim, Bright, Shining, ★ Gold). A roll picks rare (`duckRareChance`), then mid (`duckMidChance`), then the other exotics.
- Ducks: every Dark Remnant that is not a duck adds `duckLuckStep` (1%) to Duck chance, every classic duck adds it to Exotic chance; a hit rebases it. The luck is saved with the inventory (`luck`), so it carries across surfaces and days.
- Every item is a discovery (`dc_si_<id>`, Discoveries tab). One-time upgrades are kept as the discovery `up:<id>`; purse-cap upgrades raise `invMaxFor`.

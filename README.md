a discovery based visual art experience

## Players
- Several players can be saved in one browser, each with their own save (map, items, ducks, letters, souvenirs, name, buoy). Player 1's keys are as they always were; player n's are `p<n>:`-prefixed, in localStorage and the IndexedDB session (`content.js` does it for every page, so nothing else needs to know). The setup (tuners, panels) is shared.
- The title screen: **Continue** asks which player when more than one has a map saved (name and day; Shift-click it to ask with only one). **🗑 Delete runs…** there switches delete mode on: click a run, then click it again, and that player's saves and everything kept for them (recordings, the surface they left) are gone (`ilProfileDel`, `IDB.wipe`); **New player** starts another (their name is asked on the map). The player chosen is kept for the tab (or `?profile=n`).

## Chroma, The Point, effect radii and read-outs
- **The Point** is the map's centre (it was the source): a big white light in the title splash's chromatic light, its colours moving and its vibrancy ever changing (`chromaGlow`, `chromaRGB`, `chromaVib` in `shared.js`; it lights the shops white tinged with the colour it is passing through). Remnant and relic glows take the very same starlight (`chromaGlow`: its lobes drifting round a white heart and blending toward white; Bright, Neutron, Pulsing and every relic; a Pulsing one keeps its beat, a Magnetic one its two colours and only breathes; Dim and Dark keep theirs). One set of tuners: `chromaTurnS`, `chromaBreatheMs`, `remChromaR`, `remChromaA`.
- **Effect radii** show what they do: arrows inside each ring, in its colour, drawn as the velocity read-outs draw theirs (`drawEffectFlow` → `drawPredictedPath`): out (THROW), spiralling out (FLING), round (ORBIT), spiralling in (WHIRL), in (CATCH), brought to rest (SNARE), out / in for a Neutron enlarging / shrinking; their length is the colour's force K (`effFlowN`, `effFlowLen`). A ring that changes scale eases to it (`radEase`: `effRadGrowMs`, `effRadShrinkMs`, with the nova's tuners), a pigment's from 0; a Pulsing beam keeps its own animated pattern.
- **Hover read-outs** (`drawHoverExtras`): Class names a duck or a container (Crate, Cache, Case, Gate, Glass) under the cursor; Effect radii show the cursor's own reach: a dab's (Touch rad, or Dynamism rad with a Neutron), what it would collect, and a double click's (to the farthest of the Max shining nearest it would take).
- **The Guide's math** and an event's facts: every value names the variable(s) it comes from and their tuners when pointed at; a number from none says it is hardcoded (`tvE`, `tvHTML`).

## Mass: one Dark Remnant mass
- Every Point outcome comes from one tuner, **Dark Remnant mass** (`darkMass`, Tune → Gameplay → Dark Remnant — Behavior); nothing else sets a threshold (`blackMult`, `brightMassMax`, `riskWarnMass` and `riskDangerMass` are gone).
- **That mass**: a Point dies into a Dark Remnant, with a nova of its mass. **Half**: a Point dies into an exotic remnant with its nova; two meeting make a Dark Remnant with a nova of both masses. **A quarter**: a Point is at its max radius and dies into a Bright Remnant with its nova; two meeting make an exotic remnant (Magnetic, Neutron or Pulsing, as `chooseActiveRemnantType` resolves it) with a nova of both masses, or a Dark Remnant once the two together reach the full mass (`triggerActiveRemnantNova`). A Point made at max radius (`makeMaxBrightPoint`) has a quarter.
- Risks: WARNING from a quarter, DANGER from half (`riskOf`).

## The cursor
- **The pen's nib** (the plain cursor: nothing held, not collecting): the Fountain Pen's nib, its tip at the ring pointing to the centre, lying to the bottom left at 45°. Its cut-off shaft holds a lens, a microscope onto what is under the cursor (magnified, turned with the nib). Near an edge that would cut it off it swings smoothly to the nearest angle that fits, and back once there is room. White, and dimmed by half as much as the cursor (`cursorNib` in `content.js`; tuners **Pen nib** on / off, **Nib length**, **Nib lens rad**, **Nib zoom** under Cursor).
- **The net** (collecting; `NET`, `netKeep`, `netScoop`, `netTick`, `netDraw`, `netHoop`): pointing at a remnant, the cursor's ring becomes the net's hoop (the dot still at its centre), the net hanging down into the depth and lying back along the cursor's path, loose and wavy, longer on the screen the faster it is dragged; it comes up out of the depth and sinks away as the nib does. A **dab** splashes the net in, draws the remnant in, pauses, flips the net over in a half circle with a snap (`netSnap`: slow, then over, overshooting and settling) and collects (ripples, a splash). Whatever one flip collects is one catch: one burst, one sound, its values summed into one +n for each kind (`NET.sum`; **Full** for a kind it filled — see A full purse). Ripples, splashes and the wake are drawn bright (added light) so they show over black. A **double dab** swells the hoop out just far enough to take in every remnant of the kind clicked within **Net double ×** (3) × Touch rad, draws them in as it shrinks back to size, flips and collects (ripples at its start, its widest and its finish, and a splash). **Nothing is ever collected while the left button is held**, wiping or not: what the dipped hoop passes over is gathered into the net and rides in its bottom, out of every collision (as good as collected: it cannot escape, and a Dark Remnant does not take it), until the press ends, when the net flips and collects it all (a dab still flipping when you press again keeps its catch in the net the same way). Wiping, the hoop stands on edge; at **Net wake spd** or faster the net streams straight behind, and slower it droops and waves more until, still, it hangs as on hover (hoop still on edge). Its wake is one shape that changes with speed: the pigment's bow wake (`drawWakeShape`, the Wake tuners) with that speed as its scale. Under the surface the hoop and net are dimmed. Tuners: **Net** on / off, **Net scoop ms**, **Net double ×**, **Net ripple ms**, **Net wake spd**, **Net under α**.
- **An active item owns the feed** (`ITEM_USE`, strings `surf.use_<item>`): while an item is active the feed says which (`surf.item_active`) and, in one line, how it is used, over whatever the cursor points at (a requirement it flashes, a Pattern's mode and a Prism's preview of its target still show). **BALANCE** opens as big as the Bright Remnant's energy: none gives Balance min half-side, `brightStasisEMax` (Balance energy max) or more gives Balance half-side.
- **Tuned values are kept, defaults are not** (`saveConfig`, `T_DEF`): your config (`nebula-config`) keeps only the tuners you set away from their defaults, so a default changed in `content/tuners.csv` reaches you unless you tuned that value yourself. A config saved before this kept every value; as it loads, any value in `T_OLD` still at its old default is dropped (BALANCE cost 50 and interval 50 ms, now 1 every 400 ms as DEPTH; Net scoop ms 320; Net ripple ms 700), and it is saved again the new way. DEPTH and BALANCE can cost as little as 1 Bright / Dim every millisecond (Balance / Depth cost 1, interval 1 ms).
- **The glow layer** (`glowLow`, **Glow layer scale** `glowResScale`): the big soft effect-radius glows are drawn at a fraction of full size into a layer of ⌈W × scale⌉ × ⌈H × scale⌉ pixels. It is cleared whole in its own pixels and laid back at exactly its scale (its rounded-up last row and column lie past the screen's edge), so a window size that does not divide evenly leaves no part-cleared row: that row used to keep every glow that reached it, a stain along the bottom edge whenever the window was not full screen.
- One cursor everywhere (`gameCursor` in `content.js`): the system cursor never shows, in any state, menu or card (the title screen, the map's menus and shops, the surface's Guide, cards and panels). Over the surface's scene and dock the surface draws its own; everywhere else the same ring is drawn over everything. Its click reaction is the surface's on every page: a press shrinks it in, held it flares past rest, a release eases it back, a right-click punches it out (`cursorClick*`).
- On the map, a click leaves a little ripple, a double click a splash, and a click-swipe (a drag) ripples and a wake behind it. A click that does nothing to a hex flashes it like tapped glass (with its tap), and the feed flashes with why. Every ripple (a click, a splash, a duck, a carried duck's wake) also bends the hexes it passes over, whatever state they are in (`rippleBend`, with the other Ripple tuners every water shares; the ground shader's `uRp`). Tapping a shop's window or door glass flashes its glass white, the same as a hex's (no ring at the cursor); the fifth tap in a row still shakes it.
- Its trail on the ground (`cursorStain`) is lit in the colour of the light at that time of day (`DUCK_SUN`: warm at dawn and dusk, white at midday, blue at night), not always white.
- The trail and its stain (`cursorTrailRecord`, `cursorStain`) live in `content.js` with the cursor, so every page has them: a surface (under its particles), the Surface Map's ground and the title's water. Its colour is the light's hue at full strength (never dimmer at night).
- A menu button's bracketed context ("Quit to main menu (saves this surface)") is not on the button: pointing at it says it in the feed (`parenHints`, each page's `ilHint`).

## The feed (the Surface Map)
- One place for every message: nothing is said mid-screen and no card comes up over the map; something seen for the first time on the map is said in the feed (and discovered: its card is in the Guide).
- **Pointing at anything on the map tells of it in the feed** (`feedTick`, `hexInfo`): your buoy, a shop, a duck, a bottle, a good or its trash, a plant growing or in flower, a parade, a hex and whatever lies on it (a tornado, ice, a Rift, a constellation). Pointing at something new tells of it at once, over a message that came before it. What you have not yet witnessed says so instead of naming itself: a duck you have never had is **An unknown duck**, a bottle before you have opened one, a Rift before you have seen one torn **Something you do not know yet**; a hex in the fog past your range is only **Fog** (`map.unknown*`, `map.fogged`, `map.ice` in `strings.csv`).
- Put away (its ▾), the feed still pops up for a message or an alert, then goes; its ▴ always shows (with the panels hidden too), on the map, below deck and on every surface (every dock uses the same arrow and setting: `ilDockArrow`, `ilDockMin` in `shared.js`); below deck, what you point at is the feed's hover line, as on the map (it never pops a put-away feed up); pointing into its place below the items brings it back up while you are there.
- Status is only as wide as its title, buttons and tabs, with a margin; its pages fit that.

## Parades (the Surface Map's lake)
- When you have enough ducks of one kind (a Colour, Size or Material: `mapParadeMin`, 4), they come out in a parade, once a day for each kind: one of each and the classic one, in a line like ducklings behind their mother, all the way round the spill just off its shore. At most `mapParadeMax` (9) swim in it: with more, a run of them in their order from a random one. Each surfaces slowly as it sets off, one after another (a submarine coming up while it moves on, `mapParadeRise`), and goes down the same way at the end. Size goes biggest first (in a full one, the Big Head last, behind the smallest); otherwise the special one leads (Rainbow, Gold), the other specials next, then the rest. While it passes no other duck or bottle comes in. A touch spins one and a click dunks it, but none of them ever leaves the line. Pointing at one says whose parade it is and who is in it.
- **Parade Day** (a dim fortune): no other duck or bottle comes; your own ducks (as many as you have, nine at most) parade on the hexes themselves, two to a hex, winding ring by ring through every hex, one hex further each time of day. Nothing crosses a hex they are in, as with a Rift.
- Lake ducks and bottles: a fast brush pushes one off the way it was hit (and only that); a slow one spins it where it is. When its time is up it heads straight off the screen (if it is still in view long after, it sinks away): nothing vanishes in view. They bump into each other like points on a surface, pushed apart and turned.

## Playing together (the Surface Map)
- **Menu → Play together** opens a room (PeerJS; `?net=bc` plays across two tabs of one browser); its link (`Surface_Map_HUB.html?join=…`) is in the menu and copied. Up to three friends join, any time, each with their own name, buoy and items: their own save, so what they had is theirs. The host's map is the one played; a friend joins on a free hex beside the source, and everyone's buoy floats on their hex.
- **Turns** go round each time of day in a snake (1 2 3 4, then 4 3 2 1; the bar under the time shows the order, whose turn in bold). On your turn: use as many items as you like, enter your hex's surface, then end your turn by **moving** or **Rest**. When everyone has had theirs, the time of day moves on. Off your turn you can look round from your own view (and play with your lake).
- **Surfaces**: whoever enters plays it in their own game (their items, their rules), and everyone else watches it (its frames, through the host). A player on the **same hex** shines and collects in it with them, with their own heat, and what they collect is theirs (it goes to their own save); a player elsewhere can only watch. A friend's surface cleared or torn is played on the host's map.
- **Shared luck**: any player's fortune or Daily Wonder that changes the map or what drops counts for everyone (`MP_RUN`); ones that only change your own items (three at a time, an Endless item) stay yours. Anything one player discovers (Guide entries, events, a Card of Secrets' reveal) is discovered by everyone who has not yet.
- **Items, from anywhere**: watching someone's surface you can use your own items on it (Depth, Balance, Magnetic, Neutron, Pulsing, Spindle: pick one under the picture, then click), on whichever hex you are; it costs you, field upkeep included.
- **Lakes, bottles and letters** are each player's own: a bottle shines for you only when what is in it is new to you, and opening one takes nothing from anyone else's.
- The host plays a friend's actions on its map (`mpAct`: from their hex, with their items; the cost comes off their own); the friend's own map is left untouched while they play.

## Your buoy, Status
- **Your buoy** (`buoyModel` in `shared.js`, drawn on the map by `buoyDraw`): a float in your colour (`BUOY_COLS`: the six effect colours, one between each, white, greys and black) half under the surface of the hex you stand on, reflected in it. It bobs, rocks (`mapBuoySway`), turns slowly, ripples the water, and turns and rocks a little more when the cursor touches it. Your name is on a framed 3D sign on its mast, lit from within, and a can light sits on top in a yoke, high on the mast so looking down it clears the name sign, turned to point where its light goes (`buoyCanModel`): its beam (`buoyBeam`, a faint cone drawn in 3D from the can, as wide as its lens where it starts) lights a spot on the surface itself, in the ground's own shader (no glow, no edge line): colours there brighten while black stays black, white takes a white sheen, shops and your buoy in it are lit by it as a real light (a cone from the can, crisp inside and soft to its rim); never over a Dark Remnant (the reflection stops at each one; `mapShopReflect`, 0.7). By day a shop's sign, lamp and lit room are dimmer (about half at midday, full in the evening) and its glass shows the street more, stretched away from it as light at a slant is, circling the hexes next to it clockwise (`mapSpotMs`) and swinging onto the hex you point at when you could move there or use the item in hand on it. A move sails it over with a wake; a String fires it there like a Point. Entering a surface, it is dunked the whole way while the camera comes over the hex, then turns to look straight down with the hex filling the frame, then goes down into it, and the surface fades in (`mapEnterMs`; the clean loading screen only if the surface is not drawn yet); coming back out is the same, backwards. Playing together, everyone's view does it with the player going in. The can light points straight up while it sinks or rises (and goes out under), and on a quick dip when you click it for Status. It sinks to sleep when you Rest at Midnight and bobs up in the morning. Coming back out of a surface you did not clear, it bobs up where it went down; out of one you cleared (bright, dark or a Rift), it stays under until you pick the hex to move to, and comes up there (`buoy.under`, `buoyGo`). A click or Space made while your move is still playing out (its sail, the light's change of time, a dive, the buoy bobbing up: `mapBusy`) is held and played once your turn starts (`_held`, `mapPress`), so a double click can never move and dive at once. A move to a hex you can see leaves the camera where you put it; one out of view (and a scene change: a new day, a Rift's throw, right-click) brings it to the buoy, unless you take the view (drag or zoom) on the way; a scene's own camera move cannot be taken. Every scripted camera move is slow and smooth: following your buoy, framing it for Status and back to your hex ease through one critically damped spring (`camSpring`, `mapCamEase` 2.4 s: it starts gently and never overshoots, the same at any frame rate); a scene's own moves (the day's end, back from Status) are eased with no jolt at either end and take `mapCamSlow` (1.8×) longer; the dive into a surface (`mapEnterMs`, 3 s) runs its three parts overlapping, so it never stops between them, its zoom even in scale. Your own drags and zooms are direct. Status opens on the right with the map still showing to its left: the camera puts your buoy in the middle of that space and zooms in on it as it rises (`statusCam`), and goes back to your own view when it closes. The spot light swings at one steady rate the short way round (`mapSpotTurn`), and its slow cycle round the hexes next to you carries on from wherever it is. A Rift torn under a buoy throws it to the innermost free hex (nearest first), else back to main street. Name and colour are asked on a new game, and can be changed in Status & collections → **Buoy**. The latest of each souvenir you have kept hangs on its rim. `buoys` holds every buoy on the map, yours first.
- **Buoy colours**: pointing at one (the Buoy page, or the name ask on the map) says it in the map's feed: its name, its hex and the effect colour it is, or the two it sits between (`BUOY_COL_NAMES`, `buoyColHint`). On the Buoy page the buoy's spot light is on, sweeping round as on the map: double click the water and it goes there and holds; drag across the water and it follows the cursor as fast as it can turn; a right click lets it go.
- **The spot on the water** is crisp-edged again (`mapSpotEdge`, `mapSpotA`), while the shops take the same light as a point light (`shopLights`): each surface its own look.
- **Status numbers**: every amount you hold shows in full (comma separated) over a bar of how full it is, with what it can hold in the inventory's short form at the bar's end (`stAmt`, `fmtCount`, `STATUS_CSS` in `shared.js`); duck chances, and in History each shop's buys, each day's bright and dark clears and every run's clears, are bars too (`stVal`, `stBar`). The same Status beside a shop's menu on the map.

## Build mode
- On the title screen, **~** shows **Build mode** (`chromatic_nebula.html?build=1`): a surface for making and testing scenes and prefabs. Nothing in it is saved to your game (items, ducks, discoveries).
- The surface is never cleared there, so Dark Remnants can be placed: one that forms (or is put down with Create) waits where it is, drawn, until **Test**. Remove takes it away again, and so do **Clear stage**, **Rebuild**, **New surface** and **Blank** (`clearStage` and `rebuild` empty `buildDarks`). Rifts don't form. However many wait, each size is drawn once a frame and stamped (`buildDarkCv`): a Dark Remnant's four blurred glow passes were the cost. In a **Test**, a Dark Remnant takes everything on the surface and then goes: no Surface Clear, the test plays on, and the surface keeps the colour (dark or bright) it had.
- Endless items never stop a collection: a full purse blocks nothing in Build mode (`invFull`, the one check every collect, earned item and purchase goes through), so remnants are collected and Spindles, Strings, Weaves and Pattern items are still earned there.
- Every item is there and endless (∞), everything is revealed, and the item cache shows with only its on / off. Create has a **Surface** type: click to turn the surface dark or bright (white).
- The surface's own panels are all there, as on any surface (Rebuild, Modes, Tune with every tuner, Image, the stats and the log; ~ hides them), with a **Build ▾** button first at the top left that opens Build mode's menu under it: a level's **name**, **Save** (kept in this browser, IndexedDB `build`), **Load…**, **New surface**, **Blank**, **▶ Test** (the surface plays as it would in a game: its Dark Remnant starts, a Rift can form, it can be cleared; **◼ Back to build** puts it back as it was), **Delete**, **Export** (downloads `levels.json`, every level and prefab: put it in `content/`, where the game reads it as it loads) and **Title**.
- A level keeps everything on the surface (its nebula packed small: one particle in full, the rest only what differs), its Dark Remnants and its ducks, which are collectable when it is played.
- The create tool and the item cache are no longer in a game's ~ panels: they are Build mode's.
- **Entanglement** (Build mode's second tool, beside Create, `POST_CATS` / `toolHas`): the tools for what is already there — the Inertializer, Prefab, Duplicate (Copy, Move, Repurpose) and Remove; Create keeps what makes things. Each tool comes back to the kind it was on; middle click steps its own kinds only.
- **Build menu**: a button at the top left with Modes and Tune, opening its menu as a column under it as theirs do; one of the three is open at a time (`toggleBuild`). Leaving for the title is in the Esc menu (the Build menu has no Title button).
- **Prefabs** (Entanglement → Prefab): **Select** parts (click, or drag a box, as the Inertializer: points, remnants, pigments and shapes, with their motion, and the nebula's own particles, which never move: a prefab with any of them phases in where it is put, never drifting in from an edge), then in the bar's PREFAB row give it a name, the times of day it can appear (from – to, the map's clock; across Midnight is fine) its **value** (how likely it is to be the one that appears against the others for that time; 0 never) and its phase (**appears at**: ms from the surface's start, 0 at once; **stays**: ms, 0 for good) and **Save prefab** (kept with the levels; Export writes them to `levels.json` too). The **Phaser** sets the phasing for the whole prefab (click anywhere) or for one part (click it: its own appears-at, after the prefab's, and stay, orange). **Place** phases one in where you click, at once (its list shows each one's times and value, and the value can be changed there).
- **Phasing** (every prefab and part alike, in and out: `prefabPhaseMs`): a part grows from a point, all black, then a white flash runs out from it and reveals it, real: until then it passes over everything; once real it is fully active, and moving at once if it was. Phasing out is the same, backwards.
- **By time of day**: when a surface is entered at a time of day (a map hex: the map's clock), how many prefabs appear is drawn for that time (`prefabMax<hour>`: from 0 up to it; each count as likely as (count+1)^`prefabCurve<hour>`, so up to 1 at 1.6 is none 25%, one 75%), and which by their value among those whose times include it (no repeats). Each waits for its appears-at, then a still one phases in somewhere on the surface, tending to the open space where the nebula is thinnest (`prefabOpenBias`; it can still go over the nebula), and one that moves may instead drift in from off an edge; with a stay it phases out again. The prefabs are the content files' (`levels.json`) and your own made in Build mode (kept in this browser; loaded for every surface you play), and **Test at** a time of day tries it (the feed says which will appear).
- **At an edge** (Place, Move or Duplicate within 24 px of one), it is pushed fully off the screen (`prefabEdgeBuf` px more) and waits there, shown half see-through with a yellow border and an arrow, where it will come on. When the surface is played (or tested), it drifts in, each part coming on as it crosses the edge. If its motion would never bring it on screen (still, or moving away), it is not placed, and the feed says why.

- **Image…** (or an image dropped on the surface) is laid at its own size, centred: a pixel of it to a pixel of the screen. One too big for the screen is made smaller to fit whole; none is ever stretched to fill. What it does not cover stays uncovered (`prepareImageSample`).

Kept in this browser (`nebula-presets`, `tunePresets`); a preset is applied as Load vars applies a file (`applyLoadedConfig`), and becomes the saved config. Save vars / Load vars still move a configuration between browsers.

- **Orbit, Fling and Pull together**: each spin effect holding a target keeps its own catch (`tgt._orbS`, by source: where it took hold, how far the catch has come, which way round). Which way round a target goes is decided when it is caught (the way it was moving, else its spin, else clockwise) and kept while that effect holds it, so a nudge no longer flips it (the wobble).

- **New, and seen**: what has never been used pulses gold (`.isnew`) until first used, and a new card lies face down until turned over (`.mtg.fd`), as before. Some things are new until **looked at** rather than used: an element with `data-hov` stops being new once pointed at for 0.4 s, counted by its key, so a menu redrawn meanwhile still counts and a quick sweep across does not (`content.js`). A shop's items (map shop menus) are these. Below deck, an object pointed at for 0.6 s is seen, as a click on it was. Your souvenirs and the shops in your collection lie face down when newly gained (`sv_<id>`, `shv_<shop>`).

- **Effects' first use** (events, `content/events.csv` type *Event — Effect*): the first time each effect actually does something is an event like the others, captured as you see it and kept in the Journal: each colour's effect reaching something (THROW, FLING, ORBIT, WHIRL, CATCH, SNARE: `applyForce`), ECLIPSE and CRYSTALIZE turning something, a Neutron's Enlarged / Shrunk, a Magnetic's colour, a Pulsing's pulse passed on (`statusBonus`), and DEPTH and BALANCE placed (`placeField`). A new effect gets its event by adding a row and one `fxEventOnce(id, at)` where it happens.
- **Achievements** (`content/achievements.csv`; Tune → **Achievements** sets each threshold): lifetime counters (`stat`, kept in the life log, so they merge between the map and the surface like the rest of it) reaching a threshold reach an achievement (`ach:<id>`, kept with the discoveries), said in the feed, and unlock what it names: an interface element (found), a shop (it can open: `popupFor` on the map), or a shop item (listed and sold: `shopRows`, `shopBuy`). What an achievement unlocks is locked until then (`achLocked` in `shared.js`). See `content/README.md` for the counters.

## Rifts
- **On a surface** (`riftCheck`, Rift mass in one area): it forms slowly, so there is a moment to stop it (`riftForms`): a single glass crack appears through that mass at a random angle, glowing with the Dark Remnant's pulse; while the mass there holds, every `riftStageMs` the crack grows out from its centre toward both edges by a quarter of the line's length on screen at each end (an eighth of the whole per end, per stage, over `riftCrackMs`), each new length bent by up to `riftBend`°, so the fourth stage reaches the edges and the Rift opens. Thin the mass below Rift mass and it seals back a crack every `riftSealMs` (it picks up again if the mass returns). More than one area can crack at once, but the mass held by one crack is never counted for another. Then it snaps open, faster than its wave (`riftOpenMs`), along the crack's own legs, bends and all (`riftSides`, `riftSpine`: the open Rift lies exactly where its crack ran), into a line Dark Remnant: a pure black stripe (`riftWMin`–`riftWMax` px wide, pinched to `riftPinch` × in its middle and widening toward its ends) edged with the Dark Remnant's two rings' banded, swaying, beating glow in their wide and mid blur passes (`drawRiftGlow`, `darkRingCfgs`); whatever it opens over is lost. Its surface's clear is titled RIPPED, its end line the Dark Remnant's; behind the wave the surface goes black, as behind a Dark Remnant's (no white wash: the bursts show). A wave then runs out from it on both sides, its fronts following the Rift's line, and everything is drawn straight across to it and consumed, with the Dark Remnant's bursts where it hits. It is the Dark Remnant's own collapse (`blackHole` with `.rift`: every distance is to its line, `bhVec`), so its settings govern the rest; remnants can still be collected at the last second. Never a duck.
- **On the Surface Map** (`riftCell`): the hex is gone, a scar (tapered to points at its ends and pinched thin in its middle, `riftPinch`) drawn as the surface's Rift is, a line Dark Remnant, opening as the map comes back (side by side, Rifts line up into one edge; three that touch, a triangle, and an edge beside a triangle runs into its corner): it snaps open, a ring of a wave goes out as a nova's does seen from above, and everything round it is drawn in (a bright clear it takes shatters into glowing white, a shop on it is torn to pieces, `riftFx`); and the open hexes beside it are brightened: cleared as bright clears (their value kept). A buoy on the torn hex goes under, and you choose where it comes up: past the bright fringe round the tear (a move runs over clears to the first open hex). Moves cannot cross a Rift, and a String cannot shoot through one (a straight line only: hexes behind one are not lit as targets). Scars side by side line up into one edge; three that all touch merge into a triangle (`riftsDraw`). They are gone at Midnight (the new map).

- **Recycling plants** (`plants`; BRiGHT IDEAS sells the **seed**, 🌱 in the dock): planted on a Dark Remnant clear beside you. Its first trash sets its material; each piece grows it a stage (`plantStage`: the tip of its stem, a stem out of the water, a second stem with its leaves grown out, a bud), **Plant bud trash** (4) for the bud (more than 4: a stem more for each), and it takes trash until **Plant bloom trash** (5). Models (`plantModel`, `plants/*.svg` laid as in `RecyclingPlant.svg`): paper is the drawing itself, flat and white as the paper duck; metal, glass, plastic and fabric are true 3D models after the drawings (`plantMesh`, built with `meshKit`'s `tube` and `surf`, smooth-shaded: every corner carries its own normal, so no seams show): a tapering stem with an S in it, broad leaves in pairs, each pair a quarter turn on from the last (more pairs the more trash its bud needs), cupped and drooping, a midrib and veins raised on them (fabric's edges stitched); a round bud with its petals' tips showing; in bloom, two rings of cupped petals round a glossy black dome ringed in glowing orange (`plantc`, its own part, drawn first in the same depth as the plant: `with`). Each is drawn in the duck shader's own material (`PLANT_MAT`): **metal** as a Metal Duck and **glass** as a Glass Duck or a bottle (the scene reflected in them, the lights glinting; glass see-through), **plastic** in clear plastic (material 14: a duck's glossy plastic you can see through, most solid at its edges), **fabric** woven (`weaveTex`: threads over and under, each round, laid unbroken across each leaf and petal, their relief its bump, `pb` in model units).
- **The collapse's singularities** (the map: `spillOf`, `singEarn`, `singDraw`; content/singularities.csv): at Midnight everything falls into the source and shapes the next spill — each bright clear +1% of its mass, each Rift −1% (summed, ±20% at most: 36.00 is 43.20 at best, 7.20 a hex at Twilight), and every new surface of the next day is laid with 2 CRYSTALIZE particles a bright clear and 2 ECLIPSE particles a Dark Remnant clear (100 clears each at most; `spillTint`). Each Dark Remnant (a merged three as one, a merged hex of seven as one) deals a **singularity** card into your deck: tier 1, a merged three 2, a hex 3, and a flower left on it one better, in its material (`singCard`: a good fortune's effect with a dim fortune's catch, both worse the higher the tier; a flower's card has its material's own flavour — paper items, metal riches, glass luck, plastic ducks, fabric trade — where a Dark Remnant's is any). Your deck lies on the desk below deck, as deep as it is. At the new day, your deck deals three (the best and the flowers' first, at random among them if more): you must pick one — it holds while you are awake today (`run.sing`: `runFx`, `runBad`) — and the rest are discarded. **Toys** on your mounts (Dim's): **Kaleidoscope** a card more, **Pinwheel** a card a tier better, **Yo-yo** lets you pass, **Snow globe** earns a reshuffle each Midnight (the discards go back in once the deck runs out); two of a kind, or one on a doubled riser, count twice, and a toy the collapse wrecks still counts that night. ## The Guide
Every entry is a card in one format (name, a live demo, page and Tier I–V, then its details and live math, each behind its own discovery). **Nothing in the Journal is given**: every entry, How to play included, is face down until you have done or witnessed what it is about in play — its event (`RULE_EV`), or `ruleLearn` where it happens (a dab, a wipe, a mop, a double dab, heat, a right click, a colour change, each effect shining, a shape on a surface, collecting, the menu, the item cache, an exotic duck; on the map `hubLearn`: moving, entering a hex, an item used on the map, looking round, buying, inviting a friend), said in the feed (`guide.learned`). Its details, more demos and math still open with Dim. Pages: **Discoveries** (how to play, how to clean up, and the events witnessed), **Taxonomy** (every kind of thing and what it becomes, drawn with the game's own glyphs; its cards below), Items, Shapes, Surface Map, **Shops** (How shops work on top: main street, pop-ups, buying; then each shop's card, only its sign's shape until it has opened, with what it does and a tab for each currency it takes), Ducks, Glossary. (Status, your ducks, letters and souvenirs are in **Status & collections**, below.) **Event capture**: the moment an event first happens (where it happened: a pigment, a Point, a remnant, a relic, a duck, a Dark Remnant, a Rift), the game does not stop at once: a faint frame with sparkles follows it, wrapping with it, brighter as it starts, the card's own wide shape (`evAspect`), kept small round what it leaves (a remnant, even after a big nova) and large only for big events (`EV_FRAME`: small, large, the whole screen for a Rift; `evFrameS/L`), the game slows by `evSlow` (with the novas' slows) and the scene is recorded as the game's own state, not pictures (`evRecord`: every `evSnapMs`, `evPreMs` before it, `evCaptureMs` of it; across an edge it is stitched as the screen wraps). The frame starts at its full size and closes in on its subject (`evZoomK`: `evZoomIn`, `evZoomInMs`; every event but a whole-surface one), and everything that is not its subject or what its subject came of (the Point a remnant formed from, the two that merged, a Pattern's Points: `fireEvent`'s `src`) is dimmed the way an armed tool dims what it cannot act on (`evDim`, `toolInvalidDimActive`, `invalidTargetOpacity`: the nebula, the other points, ducks and goods; only how they are drawn, never how they move or meet). **A capture is a nature shot, and a bad one is not kept** (`evFollow`, `evLostEnd`, `evCapTick`): it is cancelled if its subject stops existing before the recording ends (it died into something else, or something took it), if what happened does not last through it (`EV_HOLD`: a shining effect switched off or no longer acting on at least one thing, which `applyForce` marks; a touched thing no longer turned, a status or a field gone, a chase, an orbit or a stillness broken, the Dark Remnant gone), or if its subject is covered — anything over it or under it but what its own effect has hold of — for longer than `evLostMs`, or as the recording ends. A cancelled capture's frame shatters into sparks as any edge going away does (`spawnEdgeSparks`): something was happening and it did not finish. Nothing is kept of it and the event is not discovered: it can be witnessed another time (an effect's, after `evCaptureMs`). Its page's picture is your own moment replayed live, your journal of discoveries (`evCapSave`, `evReplayMount`: saved with your player). A recording plays in a copy of the game out of sight, and its card shows a view of it (a canvas): on its subject wherever it goes (the copy follows it as play did, `evFollow`), the surface joined round it across its edges and corners, so a subject at an edge sits in the middle and one that crosses an edge is never lost; it closes in and dims as the capture did, plays the moment before the event at the speed it happened (and a quietly recorded one with no slow at all), slows to a pause where its capture ended and holds there (`evReplayTail`) before it starts again. How it ended is kept with it as a still (`evStill`): what its small card shows at rest, in the hand, the desk and the Journal, and what its card shows until the replay plays. (So that the surface does join: the nebula's blur, a remnant's chromatic glow and a moving point's glow now carry across the edges as their objects do.) The whole surface is recorded (everything on it as it was, the nebula included, so what comes into the frame or leaves it, and what acts on it from outside, plays out too), and its frame keeps on its subject over it, wherever it goes from start to end, and when the event happened at a place, it takes up what is new there (`evAdoptR`: a remnant first) and follows that; a recording played again finds its subject the same way (its path is kept; the surface recorded is as big as it needs). Recorded too: the first surface (The Spill, the whole screen), a surface cleared, In Pursuit, Unwinding, Stillness and each pattern where it was drawn, and, quietly, each colour effect the first time you see it at work (for its Journal entry). It plays as it happened: every object exactly as it was (`evState`: life left, wake, drain, timers), on the moment's own clock, in every setting it ran on (all of `T`), with your purse, upgrades and day, and your own input (`_evIn`: each press, move, release, double click, right click and wheel, at its time, the cursor with it) — so what you did then happens again. Live demos are kept for what cannot be recorded on a surface or the map, and for every mechanic you can find without playing (Dim's Carnival). **Every event is captured, with no exception** (`fireEvent`: one with no place of its own is framed over the whole surface), each over its own time (`evCapMs`: the `capture` column of `content/events.csv`, else `evCaptureMs`). Another event during one takes the frame's slow and dimming, and the first is recorded on, still framed (`evFrameDraw`). An event is discovered when its capture ends well, and only then; **no card stops play**: it is said in the status line, its recording is kept, and it is added to what there is to review in your Journal. **The Journal's slot in Home counts the events you have not read** (✦ n, `evNew`; on the map `evTodo`), and opened with any it opens at the first of them (`guideOpen`, `jFirst`); it is new until it is looked at, in any place: its card lies face down until it is turned over (`ilNew`, see The pen, and what is new). A shop is an event the first time you go in and see what it sells (`openShop`: discovered then, framed round the shop; never just for being open). An event's card on its own is only for a secret told at the Carnival (`showEventCard`); closing it eases time back in slowly (`evResumeMs`).

## Weather (the Surface Map)
- **Calm**, until a kind sets in (`wxTick`, each time of day): Windy, Rainy, Hot, Cold, Foggy — each **Weather chance** (5%), stronger (tiers 1–3) on a rarer roll, for a few times of day. Hot and Cold cancel. Rainy + Hot + Windy is a **Storm**, Cold + Rainy **Snow**, Snow + Foggy a **Blizzard**; the dock shows it (and the wind's way).
- **Day 1 is always calm** (`wxTick`; a new game clears the weather). **Every weather is drawn in the time of day's light** (`wxDraw`: `sunInk`, `shopGlowK`): rain, snow and the wind's streaks take the light's colour and dim at night, Hot is strongest under the midday sun, lightning is starker in the dark. **Fog** hazes the whole map, banks of it drifting across (with the wind when it blows), grey-white by day and the night's blue and dark at night, and is thick beyond the hexes you can target (only those are obscured); **lights glow in it**, far more in the dark: your spotlight's beam all the way down to its pool, the Point in its colours, the bright clears, the shops' lit rooms, the cursor's lamp.
- **Fog has colour and an edge that lives** (`wxDraw`): the spill's own colours drift through it, each bank and wisp its own (rich in the morning, greying as the day bears on: **Fog colour**), in the time of day's light; past your range it is thick and faintly coloured, banks swirling over it, and its edge is never a ring — dozens of wisps and billows reach in and draw back, slowly.
- **What you cannot target, you cannot see** (`fogL`, `fogSee`): past the hexes you can target the fog is solid, and whatever floats out there — ducks, bottles, goods, flotsam, ice — is not drawn (it fades out at the edge), not pointed at and not reflected.
- **Everything 3D on the map is in the scene's own lights** (`sceneLights` as `o.L3` to `drawDuckScene`, every material in the shader): your spotlight's cone, the cursor's lamp, the Point and the bright clears light ducks, plants, goods, bottles, the ice and its snow (diffuse, sheen, and glints on what shines; ice lights up from within as they pass) as they light the shops and your buoy, on top of the time of day's sun. (Fixed: the flag that says a draw's lights are real lights was inside a comment.)
- **Ice** (`iceScene` on the map, `iceModel` in `shared.js`): a floe on each iced hex (one of eight shapes per size, by the hex): a body, then layers of terraces of very uneven height (from a sliver to a tall step) each set in by its own amount and off centre, ridges running off its sides and up over its tiers as spines, grooved walls, every top uneven. **Ice side by side is one mass** (`iceMass`): each floe's base reaches right across the sides it shares with ice to meet its neighbours', the mass rides the swell as one, and the deeper a hex lies in its mass the taller and more terraced it rises, its tiers leaning up toward the mass's middle — a tall peak at the centre as it grows. **The ice is see-through** (material 15, drawn as glass is: its far walls first): what is under and behind it bent through it and tinted blue, the scene reflected off it more at a slant, the sun's and every lamp's glints, a rough surface from a three-scale noise normal map and patches of frost. **Snow** (material 16, `snowMass`) is formed by its rules over each whole mass at once, not laid on per floe: every floe's tops together as one heightfield, so a drift runs across the seam between two floes and nothing repeats from one floe to the next (its small variation is by where it lies on the lake, and every tier of every floe has its tops' swells turned and warped its own way). Its shape is rounded in a smoothing pass within itself (never across a wall) and its normals taken over a wider reach, so it shades round, never in facets. It is formed a little each frame (`snowMassStep`: its last finished snow drawn meanwhile, the first formed faster). It is one blanket as it accumulates: every surface that is not a wall covered (only the ice's walls, steeper than about 65°, show through), smoothing over the ripples and dips under it; **a cap** where a top drops away down a wall to a lower layer — its snow rounds over the edge to a soft lip rather than stopping square; and **drifts** blown into the inner corners at the foot of each step, billowing up against it but only so high (a short step they bury, a taller one they leave standing as ice) — where a drift reaches the top of its step it runs on smoothly into the snow on the layer above; where the step is too tall it stops short and the ice stands out of the snow. **Its edges are frosting**: wherever the blanket ends over a drop (the mass's outer edge, the top of a step it does not bridge) its edge is smoothed out of the grid's stairs into a flowing line and rolled over in a soft round bead that curls back in under itself, sagging into a drip here and there (none against a wall rising beside it); lit by the cool sky all over and the sun where it faces it, sparkling as the floe turns. More of it the more ice round a floe, and while it snows.
- **Windy**: a move against it drinks twice, with it at your back none (`waterDrinkMove`). **Rainy**: the barrel fills a drink each time of day; hull mounts are soggy (no use). **Hot**: every move and dive drinks one more. **Cold**: ice cubes form on open hexes (nothing moves onto them; `c.ice`) and drift off; in Snow they bind and grow, and the barrel is frozen. **Foggy**: you can only target hexes 3, 2 or 1 away (`fogR`).
- **Storm**: a tornado rides the wind a hex each time of day — it flings you to a random open hex and takes any pop-up it passes (through the Point: main street shut for the day, `wxDown`); lightning (**Lightning chance**) shuts a shop for the day, or strikes your buoy: a wreck roll, and your next move is lost.

## Sounds of the world (the Surface Map)

Made as they play (Web Audio, no files), natural rather than gamey: the makers are `SFX_MAKE` in `shared.js` (`sfxPlay`), the map's engine `ambTick` / `sfx` in `music.js`, on **Sound** and its volume.

- **Round your buoy** (`ambTick`, kept up every 150 ms): the sea's slow swell and the hiss of its crests; wind in gusts (stronger with Windy, a tornado near); rain, with drops on the deck (snow hushes everything, muffled); a foghorn far off in fog; thunder rolling after the lightning; now and then a gull on a calm day.
- **The hexes' tones**: Bright hexes near you hum a high glass chord, Dark ones a low drone, and near a Rift a tone glitches in and out and the others stutter with it (`ambTones`).
- **Below deck**: the water lapping and knocking at the hull from inside, the candle's flame and its crackle.
- **In a surface**: the map's world sits under it, quiet and muffled (as the record does).
- **What you do**: moving (water pushed aside, spray, the mast creaking), firing across, diving in (a splash, bubbles) and coming up, the hatch (a creak, the lid's thud), a shop's door bell as it opens, a catalogue opened; on the surface the Journal opening and its pages turning (`volPage`).
- Tuners (**Sounds**): `ambVol` (the world), `sfxVol` (actions), `ambTones`, `volPage`.
- **EDM sounds** (the surface; **Modes → EDM sounds** for now, kept as `il_edm_v1`; gear is to turn it on later): every note the surface plays — the same notes, never more — is played on a house track's instruments instead of the orchestra's (`edmNote`, placed in each instrument once it has chosen its notes, loudness and length): the violin's chords (and the mop's held chord) a string pad, chimes, the xylophone, bells, glissandos and collecting a house piano, the flute, the triangle, the neutron and the pulsing echoes a Rhodes, the clarinet and the dim remnant an organ, the tuba a deep bass, the horn a brass stab, the magnetic pair strings, the timpani a kick that pumps the rest (smoothly), the cymbal a ride, the glass a shaker; through a room's reverb and a gentle compressor, each as loud as what it stands in for (`EDM_LVL`). **A Dark Remnant or a Rift arriving**: its next note lands as the drop (the kick and a sub boom on that note, a crash) with the filter opening over two beats (`edmImpact`). **Too many too fast** (a Dark Remnant eating a crowd): notes of one instrument closer than its gap (`EDM_GAP`: a kick a sixteenth apart) or past `edmVoices` sounding are let go — those already sounding carry it, so it never crackles. Tuners: `volEdm`, `edmVerb`, `edmVoices`.

## Performance
- **Freezes of 100 ms+ with steady play between** were the browser's full garbage collection, most of it spent freeing canvases. Glow sprites (`glowSprite`) are now cached by size step (`qGlow`) with the glow's inner edge kept as a fraction of the sprite (in 16ths), drawn scaled to the exact size, and a full cache gives the least recently used sprite's canvas to the new one instead of clearing everything. In testing, canvases made fell from ~300 a second to ~20 and the longest collection from 137 ms to 7 ms. Avoid making canvases (or Path2D / ImageData) per frame: reuse them.
The Surface Map draws its ground on a 2D canvas and sends it to the GPU every frame, so its size is the main cost: `mapPlanePx` caps it, and its sharpness follows the zoom. While the map's window is not the one in use it draws `mapIdleFps` (8) frames a second. A hex's picture is built straight from the surface's particle map (only the cells it shows, its blur passes drawn into the picture, no full frame and no start snapshot): about 12 ms instead of 60, one a frame, so a whole map of new pictures eases in over a second or two. A hex's coverage is its value itself, to 1% (`cellCov`: a value of 0.3 is a surface 30% covered, out to full at `mapCovFull`), so a picture is rebuilt whenever a hex's value moves by 1%; however bare, its particles keep their full mass and energy. The light moves round to a new time of day over `mapSunMs` (4.5 s). The performance log keeps only the last 600 frames, 25 slowest and 400 events, so a map left open does not grow it.

## Sleep, waking and the Clock smith (Surface Map)
- A new day (and a new map) starts at Twilight with ring 1 only, 1.00 a hex, 6.00 in all (**Midnight burst rings** `mapBurstRings`, now 1); the spill spreads a ring each time of day, the same 6.00 shared out over every hex still uncleared (`redistribute`).
- **The water says why** (`waterWhy`: the dock's water, the feed and the hint): frozen (Snow) it says the barrel is frozen and how many drinks are ice, and that no light can fill it until the snow lets up (nothing about the Point or the tide); empty beside the light, that resting fills it; anchored, that the tide cannot carry you. Resting frozen or beside the light passes time where you are.
- **Rest** any time on the map and you **sleep** through to the next **Morning** (the day goes on without you, Midnight's collapse and all); after Midnight you sleep through Twilight and Dawn and wake at Morning (`sleepUntil`, `sleepStep`, `wakeH`: **Wake at (hour)**, **Wake-up call at (hour)**; **Sleep step ms** is how long each time of day takes to pass on screen). Asleep, nothing can be done, the day goes on without you, and no water is drunk. A **Wake-up call** (Clock smith, 150, bought the day before) wakes you at **Dawn**.
- The **Alarm clock** (Clock smith, 5000, kept: `up:alarm`) turns a Rest before Midnight into a nap (**Nap length**, 2 times of day) rather than a sleep to the next Morning. (Out of water, Rest is the tide's, as before.)
- **A time of day bought** (Clock smith): you do not move; the spill is as it would be at that time, a ring more for each time of day on, a ring fewer for each back (`timeShift`). Cleared hexes and shops stay, out in the dark when the spill no longer reaches them, and can still be sailed to.
- **Yesterday / Tomorrow, this time**: you do not move (no wreck to risk) and the map stays as it is laid out; only the day, and with it the fortunes, change (`timeTravel`).

One of each: a better rank moves the shop to the new clear and the old one is torn down. A Dark Remnant clear that earns one opens it on the nearest open bright hex or free spot in the source hex. Every turn, each cleared surface that earned a pop-up and has none open tries again (a hex a shop moved out of, a dark clear that found no room, a holiday). Every shop is drawn at main street's scale (`mapStreetSize`); the two-storey stores of ranks 2 and 3 are drawn larger (`mapShopUpScale`, 1.7) so their doors match main street's. Which opens first: the DUCK SHOP, then the best-ranked exotic one (NEUTRINOS, MAGNETS, the PULSE; a tie at random), then BRiGHT IDEAS, last Dim's.
- **INFINITE LINE** (on main street, where the Gift Shop stood; `line` in `content/shops.csv`, sign `shops/sign_line.svg`), Dawn – Dusk, for Bright (its name plate and flag options for Magnetic): the nib. **Nib shine** (how many things shine at once, `shineMax()`: the **Nib shine (base)** tuner, 1, or 5, 15 and 30 bought in order, `fx.cap.shine`); each tier opens a gesture's upgrade (`fx.heat`, `heatK`): **Light wipe** (5), **Quick double dab** (15), **Easy mop** (30), each heating the nib half as much. **Ink** (Dim by the lot, for Bright: XACT change's Dim, moved here), **Deeper Well** (DEPTH: the pouch's Dim is used only once it is bought) and how many Prisms, Knots, Lenses, Keys and Gates you hold (10, then 50, then 99).
- **BRiGHT IDEAS** (the net and what it brings in): the net starts with the dab alone. **Swoop net** collects by wiping, then **Big hoop** by double dabbing (its reach: **Net big hoop reach ×**), then **Really big hoop** (**Net really big hoop reach ×**); one tier opens the next. Without its gear the net takes only Bright and Dim: a **Metal strainer** catches Magnetic, a **Vast mesh** Neutron, an **Electric fence** Pulsing (`netCan`). **Processing** (`remnantValue`): without it a Bright or Dim Remnant is worth 1; **Bright processing** makes it 10 × its energy; **Magnetic / Neutron / Pulsing processing** (each after its net) counts what an exotic was infected with (each stacked status); a relic is worth 10 × unprocessed, the full value processed, and **Relic refinery** × more again (**Relic refinery ×**). The **Trolling net** (a hull mount, `fx.troll`): sailing across bright clears, each pays its value × **Trolling net ×** in Bright (`trawl`). **Freeze frame** (BALANCE: the pouch's Bright is used only once it is bought; HARMONY, DYNAMISM and RHYTHM the same with their shops' pouch items, `ITEM_BUY`), the chest's compartments, and how many Spindles, Strings and Weaves you hold (10, then 50, then 99).
- **Shop buildings** (`shopModel`, `shopExtra`, `pointPole` in `shared.js`): every outside wall is painted in the shop's colours (its sign's, else its glass's; a sign with no colour takes two of the spill's) in a pattern of hexes of its own — a honeycomb, bands of hexes, medallions or a mosaic — over a plinth, under a frieze of little hexes. **Signs are luminous** (**Sign brightness**: their bright parts glow, their dark board stays dark so they read; a softer neon halo), with **a ring of bulbs** round each lit sign — **chasing round only to say there is something new** (its grand opening, never yet been in, or an item that opened today: `shopNew`; else they shine steady), following the sign's own shape (round, a hex, a board: its outline found from its picture). **Every shop flies a flag** from a staff at the back of its roof — the one flag, the same as your buoy's (`flagFly`, `flagTex`): its whole hoist edge on the staff, clipped at head and foot; calm, it droops from the hoist, its fly falling in soft folds; in wind it stands out (straighter the stronger: `buoyWx` carries the wind's strength), waves crossing it — a main one running to the fly, a faster one, one slanting back across, a ripple — swelling and easing in gusts; in rain it hangs sodden; in snow it is frozen in the shape the day's wind gave it, under a heavy white rime that veils its design (thickest at its edges, frost ferns, glints: `flagWx`), icicles hanging from its foot and fly — each shop in its own pattern and colours. **In Snow, snow lies on their roofs, along the tops of their signs and on their window sills**, its edges rolled over like frosting (`snowSlabs`). **The great flagpole at the Point** stands on the source hex's shore, out from between INFINITE LINE and the Clock smith: a hexagonal plinth, a tall tapering pole, a gold ball, and the Point's own flag (black, the six effect colours running through it, its infinity in white light).
- **Shelves** (`shelfCard`): a shop's items stand on shelves as cards, four to a shelf, two shelves in view and the rest scrolled to (the planks scroll with them): each its name, the item itself turning slowly (`itemModel`, drawn by `shelfSpin`: a collectable model of what it is, by its form (`itemForm`; `model` in `content/shop_items.csv` sets one by hand): a mounted thing as it looks on the buoy, a float, mast or light as fitted, a record, tape, battery, barrel, fridge, bottle, walkman, the tools and items (spindle, weave, prism, knot, lens, key, gate), pen, pouch, chest, jar, net hoop, bin, scoop, seed packet, ticket, deck, clover, clocks, bell, hourglass, the gadgets, the loom, a constellation or shape, souvenirs; a duck item a duck; **Shelf item spin**), and pointed at, its live demo of what it does: a **drawing** where it has one (`ITEM_DRAW`, chosen by `itemDraw`, played on the card: white ink and glowing pigment as the Journal draws — the nib among the pigments its shine now holds, heat filling at half, the net's swoop or hoop, a constellation or shape drawn edge by edge, a placement on the centre or sectors, a pouch or chest filling past its old limit, processing, an exchange, speed, crossing a hex, a wreck held, a clock turned, batteries, water, a plant growing, trash netted, cards drawn, a record playing), else its live scene (`demo` in `content/shop_items.csv` names one, which also wins over the drawing; else `itemScene` picks one; `docs/shop-live-demos.md` lists each item's model and demo); what it does, and under it its price and its stock. Three kinds (`shelfKind`): an **upgrade** (yours for good: Owned), **gear** (1 in stock; Out of stock while it is yours, back on the shelf at its price if a wreck takes it) and **goods** (a deck as deep as its stock, or Always in stock; Out of stock when none are left). A card's words say only what it does in the game, never how shops price or stock it.
- **A surface tool's card** (`shelfSk`: an item whose demo is a surface scene — the nib's shine and gestures, the net and its hoops and gear, the sewing gear (Needle, Spool, Loom), Spindles, Strings, Weaves, Prisms, Knots, Lenses, Gates, Keys, the constellation and shape upgrades): at rest, **its demo's ending drawn exactly as the Journal draws a page's picture** (`evSketch` → `skStrokes` → `inkStrokes`, inked in on the Journal's paper, seeded by the item); pointed at, **the demo itself plays, the tool in use** (`ITEM_SCENE`, `itemScene` in `shared.js`). An upgrade's demo owns only it and the upgrades it needs (`itemChain`; a scene's name is `<scene>[:<arg>][|<gear>,…]`, `sceneGear` on the surface), so it shows what that one adds: **Nib shine** dabs along a snake of more pigments than it can hold — only the newest 5, 15 or 30 shine and the shining runs on off its end (`shine`); the **Swoop net** sweeps every Bright it passes and leaves the Dim, the **Big hoop** takes the ring its hoop reaches, the **Really big hoop** the next ring too, a ring past both left (`net:<id>`); the **Metal strainer**, **Vast mesh** and **Electric fence** catch their kind (`netx:<kind>`); the **Needle** draws a point's laps round a still one (`orbit`), the **Spool** a chase held (`chase`), the **Loom** a Weave's star (`weave`); the gestures' upgrades heat the nib half as much (`wipe`, `dbl`, `mop`). An ending not yet known is taken by playing the demo once out of sight (one at a time, while the shop is open: `shelfSkNeed`, `shelfSkTick`) and kept with the Journal's (`evsk`). Removed: their old flat drawings (`ITEM_DRAW` shine, heat, net, netx, constel, shape, give); `skStrokes` moved to `shared.js`.
- **Filters and order** (the icon buttons under a shop's currency tabs: `SHOP_FLT`, `SHOP_SORT`; `shopFilt`, kept for every shop in this browser as `il_shopfilt_v1`): toggled on and off, any one on within a group and every group at once — stock (▣ in stock, ∅ out of stock), kind (⇧ upgrades, ⚙ gear, ◔ consumables and resources), mount (⚓ hull, ▭ deck, ╫ risers, ⊤ mast, ◎ nest, ⌂ below deck: the Gadget Shelf's gear, the barrel, cooler and fridge, souvenirs) and ✓ what you can afford. Each shows only while it tells the open tab's items apart: one every item there fits, or none does, is hidden (and counts for nothing) until one does; the order stays while there is more than one item. The last button turns the order: what opened today first, cheapest, dearest, by name. Pointing at one says how many on the tab fit it.
- **Unlocks wait a day** (`unlockDay`, `ready`, `fresh`): an upgrade bought or an achievement reached is stamped with the day it came, and what it opens (the next item in its line, an item or a shop an achievement opens) shows from the next day — first: it leads its shelf (NEW), and a shop it opens pops up before any other.
- **Tune → Shops** (`shopEditRows`; `shopEditSet` in `shared.js`, kept in this browser as `il_shopedit_v1`): every item's name (as it shows everywhere; emptied, its own again), shop, price, what it is paid in, its stock each run, the pop-up rank it needs and the item before it; Reset shops puts `content/shop_items.csv` back.
- **Boxes** (off the lake, or in a surface) can hold anything (`boxItem`): something their shop sells, free (**Box item chance**), another commodity, full or trash, even a treasure chest (**Box commodity chance**), else their remnants. Their shop's sign covers nearly all of each side.
- **A shop's sign adrift lies flat on the water**, its brand and its lights facing the sky, rocking on the swell (`signFlat`).
- **A bottle is one bottle**: the message in a bottle, opened, is glass trash (`openBottle`: its cork out, it floats on as a `bottle` good for the net to scoop), and glass trash off the lake or on a hex is that same bottle without its cork (`goodBottle`: drawn, sized and floating as the message in a bottle, `drawBottleScene`); on a surface a glass bottle is that bottle's glass too (`duckModel`: `good:bottle:` → `bottleModel`). Removed: the separate painted green trash bottle.
- **The Gift Shop** is a pop-up now: it opens on a bright clear where nothing was collected (no thresholds: `tally` in `content/shops.csv` is what a pop-up counts, its currency when empty). **CUSTOM HULLS** counts Bright too: its ranks are a 4th, 5th and 6th tier past BRiGHT IDEAS' best (100,000, 1,000,000, 10,000,000).
- **Stock** (`stock` in `content/shop_items.csv`; empty: no limit): how many of an item its shop has each run. Every purchase is kept with the hour it was made (`run.sold`, the surface's run state), and `shopLeft` counts only those at or before the map's present hour: a new run is fully stocked, and turning the clock back to before a purchase (the Clock smith: an earlier time of day, or yesterday) puts it back on the shelf — buying it then replaces the later purchase. Sold out, its row says so and cannot be bought; pointing at a stocked item says how many are left.
- **Shop signs**: each is the shape of its own outline and no more (`signPrep`: a black ground in its drawing is no part of it; its board is what the outline encloses, or for one with none — lettering, a ring of lamps — the hull round its marks), on a board with a little thickness (slices of its shape, front to back).
- **Where a pop-up stands**: in the back half of its bright hex (`popSpot`, `mapPopBack`: 0.42 of a hex), so a buoy can be on that hex (or sail into it) without touching it: a buoy on a hex with a pop-up floats as far forward (`buoySpot`, everyone's).
- **The Surface Map's live demos** (a card's art: `hub:<scene>`) frame what each is about, not the middle of the map: its hexes, or its ducks and bottle, fitted to the card's own frame (`SCENE_FOCUS`, `sceneLook`, looked at again as it plays). A shop's card plays its own demo (`hub:shop:<id>`): the shop, its list opened (scaled to fit the card) and something bought, then the whole building.
- **Shop hours** (`content/shops.csv` open / close): both are times of day the shop is open (the first and the last: one that closes at Midnight is open on the last turn of the day). BRiGHT IDEAS and Dim's Dawn – Dusk, MAGNETS and NEUTRINOS Morning – Evening, the PULSE Afternoon – Midnight (the DUCK SHOP: always). A pop-up is lit and open only in its hours. A clear can only bring one that is open at that time of day: one it earned that is closed is **missed** (said in the feed; that hex will not try it again) and is **Coming soon** on main street straight away: it takes the source hex's spot of the oldest pop-up there (`hubPut`: first in, first out, whatever its rank; one of each: not if one already stands at that rank or better), standing closed with a lit COMING SOON sign and its name (`soonSign`, readable at any time of day), and the next day it opens there (`popSoonOpen`). Earned later the same day after all, it is no longer coming. A new pop-up waits while its hex's clear plays, then rises out of the bright (`shopRise`, `mapShopRiseMs`) and turns on and opens once it is up.
- **Dim's Carnival of Discovery** (its secrets), each once a day: a Card of Secrets (50 bright: pick one of three face-down cards; what it hides, an event or Guide entry you have not found, is discovered for good and its own card is shown; gone from the menu once nothing is left), Fortune Telling (200: a good fortune named for a duck or a dim one, and every fortune is for tomorrow: it takes hold as the next day starts, `fortuneStart`), a Daily Wonder (2,000: keep one of three bonuses with a catch, at once, through the time of day before the one it was drawn at tomorrow: drawn at Morning, it lasts to the end of Dawn the next day, `run.bonus.thru`). Status says both plainly: today's fortune, tomorrow's, and the time the wonder lasts through. The dim fortune **Dangerift**: after every clear of any kind (bright, dark or a Rift), a bright clear beside a Rift is removed and a dark one becomes Rift too, and so on. The draw and the card picked are shown by the surface over the map, in the one card format: what it changes in play set apart (monospace, ruled) from what is said of it (the flavour), and a live demo — a good fortune's own duck on the lake, a dim fortune's Dim or Dark Remnants (or the map rule it bends), a wonder's item at work.
- A shop's menu lists everything at once (a long list makes it taller): a tab for each thing it takes, by its symbol (named in the feed), every purse in the dock with the open tab's lit, the item under the cursor described in the feed, and Status at the right.
- **DUCK SHOP** also sells Luck Charms (+5% duck chance today, +1% on the next drop, ×10 the price each one, back to 5,000 each day) and the Lucky Duck (1,000,000, for good: base duck chances ×2, each miss +4%).
- Today's fortune, wonder and charms, and the Keys a fortune took, are the run's state in the surface's save (`run`); the **Status** tab of Status & collections (and the panel beside a shop's menu) shows them with the wallet, its caps and the upgrades bought.

- Ducks: every Dark Remnant that is not a duck adds `duckLuckStep` (1%) to Duck chance, every classic duck adds it to Exotic chance; a hit rebases it. The luck is saved with the inventory (`luck`), so it carries across surfaces and days.
- Every item is a discovery (`dc_si_<id>`, Discoveries tab). One-time upgrades are kept as the discovery `up:<id>`; purse-cap upgrades raise `invMaxFor`.

# infinitelight
a discovery based visual art experience

## Play
Served as a static site (GitHub Pages: Settings → Pages → Deploy from branch `main`, folder `/`).
`index.html` opens the title screen.

**New nebula versions must keep the name `chromatic_nebula.html`** — the map and title screen load it by that name. When you replace it, bump the `&v=` number on its `<iframe>` in `Surface_Map_HUB.html` and `TitleSplash.html` so browsers fetch the new copy instead of a cached one.

## Players
- Several players can be saved in one browser, each with their own save (map, items, ducks, letters, souvenirs, name, buoy). Player 1's keys are as they always were; player n's are `p<n>:`-prefixed, in localStorage and the IndexedDB session (`content.js` does it for every page, so nothing else needs to know). The setup (tuners, panels) is shared.
- The title screen: **Continue** asks which player when more than one has a map saved (name and day); **New player** starts another (their name is asked on the map). The player chosen is kept for the tab (or `?profile=n`).

## Chroma, The Point, effect radii and read-outs
- **The Point** is the map's centre (it was the source): a big white light in the title splash's chromatic light, its colours moving and its vibrancy ever changing (`chromaGlow`, `chromaRGB`, `chromaVib` in `shared.js`; it lights the shops white tinged with the colour it is passing through). Remnant and relic glows take the same light (`chromaGrad`: Bright, Neutron, Pulsing and every relic; a Pulsing one keeps its beat, a Magnetic one its two colours and only breathes; Dim and Dark keep theirs). One set of tuners: `chromaTurnS`, `chromaBreatheMs`, `remChromaR`.
- **Effect radii** show what they do: arrows inside each ring, in its colour, drawn as the velocity read-outs draw theirs (`drawEffectFlow` → `drawPredictedPath`): out (THROW), spiralling out (FLING), round (ORBIT), spiralling in (WHIRL), in (CATCH), brought to rest (SNARE), out / in for a Neutron enlarging / shrinking; their length is the colour's force K (`effFlowN`, `effFlowLen`). A ring that changes scale eases to it (`radEase`: `effRadGrowMs`, `effRadShrinkMs`, with the nova's tuners), a pigment's from 0; a Pulsing beam keeps its own animated pattern.
- **Hover read-outs** (`drawHoverExtras`): Class names a duck or a container (Crate, Cache, Case, Gate, Glass) under the cursor; Effect radii show the cursor's own reach: a dab's (Touch rad, or Dynamism rad with a Neutron), what it would collect, and a double click's (to the farthest of the Max shining nearest it would take).
- **The Guide's math** and an event's facts: every value names the variable(s) it comes from and their tuners when pointed at; a number from none says it is hardcoded (`tvE`, `tvHTML`).

## The cursor
- One cursor everywhere (`gameCursor` in `content.js`): the system cursor never shows, in any state, menu or card (the title screen, the map's menus and shops, the surface's Guide, cards and panels). Over the surface's scene and dock the surface draws its own; everywhere else the same ring is drawn over everything. Its click reaction is the surface's on every page: a press shrinks it in, held it flares past rest, a release eases it back, a right-click punches it out (`cursorClick*`).
- On the map, a click leaves a little ripple, a double click a splash, and a click-swipe (a drag) ripples and a wake behind it. A click that does nothing to a hex flashes it like tapped glass (with its tap), and the feed flashes with why. Every ripple (a click, a splash, a duck, a carried duck's wake) also bends the hexes it passes over, whatever state they are in (`rippleBend`, with the other Ripple tuners every water shares; the ground shader's `uRp`). Tapping a shop's window or door glass flashes its glass white, the same as a hex's (no ring at the cursor); the fifth tap in a row still shakes it.
- Its trail on the ground (`cursorStain`) is lit in the colour of the light at that time of day (`DUCK_SUN`: warm at dawn and dusk, white at midday, blue at night), not always white.
- The trail and its stain (`cursorTrailRecord`, `cursorStain`) live in `content.js` with the cursor, so every page has them: a surface (under its particles), the Surface Map's ground and the title's water. Its colour is the light's hue at full strength (never dimmer at night).
- A menu button's bracketed context ("Quit to main menu (saves this surface)") is not on the button: pointing at it says it in the feed (`parenHints`, each page's `ilHint`).

## The feed (the Surface Map)
- One place for every message: nothing is said mid-screen and no card comes up over the map; something seen for the first time on the map is said in the feed (and discovered: its card is in the Guide).
- Put away (its ▾), the feed still pops up for a message or an alert, then goes; its ▴ always shows; pointing into its place below the items brings it back up while you are there.
- Status & collections is only as wide as its title, buttons and tabs, with a margin; its pages fit that.

## Parades (the Surface Map's lake)
- When you have enough ducks of one kind (a Colour, Size or Material: `mapParadeMin`, 4), they come out in a parade, once a day for each kind: one of each and the classic one, in a line like ducklings behind their mother, all the way round the spill just off its shore. At most `mapParadeMax` (9) swim in it: with more, a run of them in their order from a random one. Each surfaces slowly as it sets off, one after another (a submarine coming up while it moves on, `mapParadeRise`), and goes down the same way at the end. Size goes biggest first (in a full one, the Big Head last, behind the smallest); otherwise the special one leads (Rainbow, Gold), the other specials next, then the rest. While it passes no other duck or bottle comes in. A touch spins one and a click dunks it, but none of them ever leaves the line. Pointing at one says whose parade it is and who is in it.
- **Parade Day** (a dim fortune): no other duck or bottle comes; your own ducks (as many as you have, nine at most) parade on the hexes themselves, two to a hex, winding ring by ring through every hex, one hex further each time of day. Nothing crosses a hex they are in, as with a Rift.
- Lake ducks and bottles: a fast brush pushes one off the way it was hit (and only that); a slow one spins it where it is. When its time is up it heads straight off the screen (if it is still in view long after, it sinks away): nothing vanishes in view. They bump into each other like points on a surface, pushed apart and turned.

## Playing together (the Surface Map)
- **Menu → Play together** opens a room (PeerJS; `?net=bc` plays across two tabs of one browser); its link (`Surface_Map_HUB.html?join=…`) is in the menu and copied. Up to three friends join, any time, each with their own name, buoy and items: their own save, so what they had is theirs. The host's map is the one played; a friend joins on a free hex beside the source, and everyone's buoy floats on their hex.
- **Turns** go round each time of day in a snake (1 2 3 4, then 4 3 2 1; the bar under the time shows the order, whose turn in bold). On your turn: use as many items as you like, enter your hex's surface, then end your turn by **moving** or **Rest**. When everyone has had theirs, the time of day moves on. Off your turn you can look round from your own view (and play with your lake).
- **Surfaces**: whoever enters plays it in their own game (their items, their rules), and everyone else watches it (its frames, through the host). A player on the **same hex** shines and collects in it with them, with their own heat, and what they collect is theirs (it goes to their own save); a player elsewhere can only watch. A friend's surface cleared or torn is played on the host's map.
- **Shared luck**: any player's fortune or Daily Wonder that changes the map or what drops counts for everyone (`MP_RUN`); ones that only change your own items (three at a time, an Endless item) stay yours. Anything one player discovers (Guide entries, events, a Card of Secrets' reveal) is discovered by everyone who has not yet.
- **Items, from anywhere**: watching someone's surface you can use your own items on it (Depth, Balance, Magnetic, Neutron, Pulsing, Spindle: pick one under the picture, then click), on whichever hex you are; it costs you, field upkeep included.
- **Lakes, bottles and letters** are each player's own: a bottle shines for you only when what is in it is new to you, and opening one takes nothing from anyone else's.
- The host plays a friend's actions on its map (`mpAct`: from their hex, with their items; the cost comes off their own); the friend's own map is left untouched while they play.

## Your buoy, Status & collections
- **Your buoy** (`buoyModel` in `shared.js`, drawn on the map by `buoyDraw`): a float in your colour (`BUOY_COLS`: the six effect colours, one between each, white, greys and black) half under the surface of the hex you stand on, reflected in it. It bobs, rocks (`mapBuoySway`), turns slowly, ripples the water, and turns and rocks a little more when the cursor touches it. Your name is on a framed 3D sign on its mast, lit from within, and a can light sits on top in a yoke, high on the mast so looking down it clears the name sign, turned to point where its light goes (`buoyCanModel`): its beam (`buoyBeam`, a faint cone drawn in 3D from the can, as wide as its lens where it starts) lights a spot on the surface itself, in the ground's own shader (no glow, no edge line): colours there brighten while black stays black, white takes a white sheen, shops and your buoy in it are lit by it as a real light (a cone from the can, crisp inside and soft to its rim); never over a Dark Remnant (the reflection stops at each one; `mapShopReflect`, 0.7). By day a shop's sign, lamp and lit room are dimmer (about half at midday, full in the evening) and its glass shows the street more, stretched away from it as light at a slant is, circling the hexes next to it clockwise (`mapSpotMs`) and swinging onto the hex you point at when you could move there or use the item in hand on it. A move sails it over with a wake; a String fires it there like a Point. Entering a surface, it is dunked the whole way while the camera comes over the hex, then turns to look straight down with the hex filling the frame, then goes down into it, and the surface fades in (`mapEnterMs`; the clean loading screen only if the surface is not drawn yet); coming back out is the same, backwards. Playing together, everyone's view does it with the player going in. The can light points straight up while it sinks or rises (and goes out under), and on a quick dip when you click it for Status. It sinks to sleep when you Rest at Midnight and bobs up in the morning. Coming back out of a surface you did not clear, it bobs up where it went down; out of one you cleared (bright, dark or a Rift), it stays under until you pick the hex to move to, and comes up there (`buoy.under`, `buoyGo`). A click or Space made while your move is still playing out (its sail, the light's change of time, a dive, the buoy bobbing up: `mapBusy`) is held and played once your turn starts (`_held`, `mapPress`), so a double click can never move and dive at once. A move to a hex you can see leaves the camera where you put it; one out of view (and a scene change: a new day, a Rift's throw, right-click) brings it to the buoy, unless you take the view (drag or zoom) on the way; a scene's own camera move cannot be taken. Every scripted camera move is slow and smooth: following your buoy, framing it for Status and back to your hex ease through one critically damped spring (`camSpring`, `mapCamEase` 2.4 s: it starts gently and never overshoots, the same at any frame rate); a scene's own moves (the day's end, back from Status) are eased with no jolt at either end and take `mapCamSlow` (1.8×) longer; the dive into a surface (`mapEnterMs`, 3 s) runs its three parts overlapping, so it never stops between them, its zoom even in scale. Your own drags and zooms are direct. Status opens on the right with the map still showing to its left: the camera puts your buoy in the middle of that space and zooms in on it as it rises (`statusCam`), and goes back to your own view when it closes. The spot light swings at one steady rate the short way round (`mapSpotTurn`), and its slow cycle round the hexes next to you carries on from wherever it is. A Rift torn under a buoy throws it to the innermost free hex (nearest first), else back to main street. Name and colour are asked on a new game, and can be changed in Status & collections → **Buoy**. The latest of each souvenir you have kept hangs on its rim. `buoys` holds every buoy on the map, yours first.
- **Buoy colours**: pointing at one (the Buoy page, or the name ask on the map) says it in the map's feed: its name, its hex and the effect colour it is, or the two it sits between (`BUOY_COL_NAMES`, `buoyColHint`). On the Buoy page the buoy's spot light is on, sweeping round as on the map: double click the water and it goes there and holds; drag across the water and it follows the cursor as fast as it can turn; a right click lets it go.
- **The spot on the water** is crisp-edged again (`mapSpotEdge`, `mapSpotA`), while the shops take the same light as a point light (`shopLights`): each surface its own look.
- **Status numbers**: every amount you hold shows in full (comma separated) over a bar of how full it is, with what it can hold in the inventory's short form at the bar's end (`stAmt`, `fmtCount`, `STATUS_CSS` in `shared.js`); duck chances, and in History each shop's buys, each day's bright and dark clears and every run's clears, are bars too (`stVal`, `stBar`). The same Status beside a shop's menu on the map.
- **On the water in Status** (the Buoy page, the Ducks pond and gallery): a buoy or duck is clipped at the waterline by the renderer itself (`drawDuck3D`'s `wat`: the water's up and its height; nothing under the water shows) and reflected under it (`drawOnWater`); a dunk splashes (`splashAdd`, `splashDraw`).
- **Buoy colours** sit in three rows of six (`BUOY_COL_ORDER`: the effect colours, the ones between, white to black; Slate added), in the light of the map's time of day; the times under them show it in another's light until Status closes (`_viewTod`, `viewSun`, `litCol`), the buoy view too.
- **Surface Map screen**: the time of day is top right, the day under it, smaller (`turnLabel`); clicking the feed moves it and the items to the top edge and back, as on a surface (`dockSide`, kept); Status over the map keeps clear of both (`dockTell` → `--dkT`, `--dkB`). The days so far are no longer in the Esc menu (Status → History has them).
- **Between pages** (`loading`, `pageReady` in `content.js`): every page opens black, with the loading screen up at once (straight on from the last page's, never fading in again: the one leaving marks the next to carry it on), and stays under it until the page says it is ready to be seen (`window.ilReady`): the title once the game's state (its tilt, its ducks), its fonts and a few frames of its water are in; the map once it is drawn (its hex pictures and shops); a surface on its own (or Build mode) after its first frames. The loading screen is built once and kept.
- **XACT change** lists each lot by the symbol of what you get, not its name (the name is said in the feed).
- **Scrollbars**: every one in the game is thin and plain, like the menus' (one rule in `content.js`, so every page has it).
- **Below deck** (click your buoy; `cabin.js`): you climb down its hatch (it has one on its deck now) into its cabin, and Status & collections opens from what is in it — the Mirror opens your buoy's Status, Storage its gear (its name and colour), the Journal the Guide; everything else says what it is (the Ink Well and the chest their exact counts); the ladder takes you back up, the camera moving down and up it.
- **Status & collections** (from below deck, or its slot in the map's dock or a surface's inventory): the same menu as the Guide, with its own tabs: **Buoy** (on the left, two thirds wide and the page's full height: your buoy big on the map's dark lake among its hexes, seen at the map's tilt (`mapTilt`) in the map's time-of-day light — drag across it to turn and rock it, click to dunk it, scroll to zoom; on the right, one column that scrolls: its name and colour, then Status: **Now** — the wallet against its caps, today's and tomorrow's fortune, the wonder and when it ends, duck luck — or **History**: what you have bought in each shop, this run day by day (how each ended: a bright or a dark collapse, or Collapse at midnight), every run there has been (`lifeLog`: runs, days, clears of each kind, kept with the purse) and this run's play-by-play (`playLog`: what the feed said and when, kept with the map)), **Ducks** (one of each type you have, at true size: a **Gallery** on the water, a **Pond** on the map's dark lake where each bobs, bobbles and turns in its own pen (swipe to spin, click to dunk, double-click to send it up high: it bobbles as it settles — a roll from side to side about its length, as on the map's lake and the title — and every dunk splashes drops of pigment), or adrift in **Space**; Invert turns any of them white; click one in the Gallery or Space to look at it up close), **Shops** (the shops you have found: click one to look at its building up close in the map's light — turn it, zoom, open or closed, a pop-up at each rank — with what you have bought there: `shopLog`, kept with the purse), **Letters** (the book of letters from the bottles) and **Souvenirs**. One viewer for every model up close (`openModelViewer`: a duck, a shop).

## Build mode
- On the title screen, **~** shows **Build mode** (`chromatic_nebula.html?build=1`): a surface for making and testing scenes and prefabs. Nothing in it is saved to your game (items, ducks, discoveries).
- The surface is never cleared there, so Dark Remnants can be placed: one that forms (or is put down with Create) waits where it is, drawn, until **Test**. Remove takes it away again. Rifts don't form.
- Every item is there and endless (∞), everything is revealed, and the item cache shows with only its on / off. Create has a **Surface** type: click to turn the surface dark or bright (white).
- The surface's own panels are all there, as on any surface (Rebuild, Modes, Tune with every tuner, Image, the stats and the log; ~ hides them), with a **Build ▾** button first at the top left that opens Build mode's menu under it: a level's **name**, **Save** (kept in this browser, IndexedDB `build`), **Load…**, **New surface**, **Blank**, **▶ Test** (the surface plays as it would in a game: its Dark Remnant starts, a Rift can form, it can be cleared; **◼ Back to build** puts it back as it was), **Delete**, **Export** (downloads `levels.json`, every level and prefab: put it in `content/`, where the game reads it as it loads) and **Title**.
- A level keeps everything on the surface (its nebula packed small: one particle in full, the rest only what differs), its Dark Remnants and its ducks, which are collectable when it is played.
- The create tool and the item cache are no longer in a game's ~ panels: they are Build mode's.
- **Entanglement** (Build mode's second tool, beside Create, `POST_CATS` / `toolHas`): the tools for what is already there — the Inertializer, Prefab, Duplicate (Copy, Move, Repurpose) and Remove; Create keeps what makes things. Each tool comes back to the kind it was on; middle click steps its own kinds only.
- **Build menu**: a button at the top left with Modes and Tune, opening its menu as a column under it as theirs do; one of the three is open at a time (`toggleBuild`). Leaving for the title is in the Esc menu (the Build menu has no Title button).
- **Prefabs** (Entanglement → Prefab): **Select** parts (click, or drag a box, as the Inertializer: points, remnants, pigments and shapes, with their motion, and the nebula's own particles, which never move: a prefab with any of them phases in where it is put, never drifting in from an edge), then in the bar's PREFAB row give it a name, the times of day it can appear (from – to, the map's clock; across Midnight is fine) and how it phases (in, out and stay ms; stay 0 = it stays) and **Save prefab** (kept with the levels; Export writes them to `levels.json` too). The **Phaser** sets the phasing for the whole prefab (click anywhere) or for one part (click it: its own in / out / stay, orange). **Place** phases one in where you click.
- **Phasing**: a part grows from a point, all black, then a white flash runs out from it and reveals it, real: until then it passes over everything; once real it is fully active, and moving at once if it was. Phasing out is the same, backwards.
- **By time of day**: when a surface is entered at a time of day (a map hex: the map's clock), by `prefabChance` one prefab whose times include that hour appears: a still one phases in somewhere on the surface (anywhere: it wraps), one that moves may instead drift in from off an edge. Its own phasing applies, so one with a stay phases out again. The prefabs are the content files' (`levels.json`); in Build mode, the ones made there too, and **Test at** a time of day tries it (the feed says which appeared).
- **At an edge** (Place, Move or Duplicate within 24 px of one), it is pushed fully off the screen (`prefabEdgeBuf` px more) and waits there, shown half see-through with a yellow border and an arrow, where it will come on. When the surface is played (or tested), it drifts in, each part coming on as it crosses the edge. If its motion would never bring it on screen (still, or moving away), it is not placed, and the feed says why.

## Rifts
- **On a surface** (`riftCheck`, Rift mass in one area): it forms slowly, so there is a moment to stop it (`riftForms`): a single glass crack appears through that mass at a random angle, glowing with the Dark Remnant's pulse; while the mass there holds, every `riftStageMs` the crack runs a quarter of the screen further (over `riftCrackMs`), a crack branching off at a slight angle each time, and when the fourth has filled the screen the Rift opens. Thin the mass below Rift mass and it seals back a crack every `riftSealMs` (it picks up again if the mass returns). More than one area can crack at once, but the mass held by one crack is never counted for another. Then it snaps open, faster than its wave (`riftOpenMs`), into a pure black stripe (`riftWMin`–`riftWMax` px wide) with long edges and nearly flat bends; whatever it opens over is lost. Its surface's clear is titled RIPPED, its end line the Dark Remnant's; behind the wave the surface whitens as a bright clear's does (only the stripe is black). A wave then runs out from it on both sides, parallel to it, and everything is drawn straight across to it and consumed, with the Dark Remnant's bursts where it hits. It is the Dark Remnant's own collapse (`blackHole` with `.rift`: every distance is to its line, `bhVec`), so its settings govern the rest; remnants can still be collected at the last second. Never a duck.
- **On the Surface Map** (`riftCell`): the hex is gone, a scar (tapered to points at its ends, like a brush stroke) in the Dark Remnant's pulsing glow, opening as the map comes back (side by side, Rifts line up into one edge; three that touch, a triangle, and an edge beside a triangle runs into its corner): it snaps open, a ring of a wave goes out as a nova's does seen from above, and everything round it is drawn in (a bright clear it takes shatters into glowing white, a shop on it is torn to pieces, `riftFx`); and the open hexes beside it are brightened: cleared as bright clears (their value kept). A buoy on the torn hex goes under, and you choose where it comes up: past the bright fringe round the tear (a move runs over clears to the first open hex). Moves cannot cross a Rift, and a String cannot shoot through one (a straight line only: hexes behind one are not lit as targets). Scars side by side line up into one edge; three that all touch merge into a triangle (`riftsDraw`). They are gone at Midnight (the new map).

## Commodities, deliveries, flotsam and treasure chests
- **Commodities** (Create → Commodity; `goodModel` in `shared.js`, drawn by the duck renderer): a can of PULSE soda (an effect colour, the PULSE's logo on its dark half), a Corner Store coffee, a can of duck (aluminium, the DUCK SHOP's logo, a pull tab), a box from any shop (cardboard, the shop's logo on its side, a plain shipping label, its seams sealed with shiny clear tape), a shop's sign (lit and blinking while its shop is open, in space or on the lake) and a treasure chest (only ever on the lake). Every one comes whole or opened (`GOOD_VARIANTS`), and a box's or sign's shop is what it holds or shows. They go into prefabs and Build levels like anything else. In space a click opens one: what it holds comes out where it is (`goodHolds`: remnants to collect, or a duck) and its packaging floats away (`goodAwayMs`); now and then one drifts in (`goodChance`).
- **Deliveries**: buying from a shop you are not at (on a pop-up's hex, or beside the Point for main street: `shopHere`) sends what it gives in a box that floats in on the lake towards your buoy; open it to get it (`shopBuy`'s `ship`, `unbox`).
- **The lake** also brings a can, a cup or a box now and then (`mapGoodMs`), and a treasure chest (`mapChestMs`): wood bound in gold with an ornate lock; a Key opens it (no challenge): the lid cracks open with a glow at its seam, its hoard of remnants and relics goes into your purse and the feed (`chestOpen`: `chestBright`, `chestRelic`), then the empty chest sinks and burns away (`mapChestOpenMs`, `mapChestBurnMs`).
- **Flotsam**: packaging left on a surface you clear never stops the clear; it surfaces with you onto a random hex (`cells[k].flot`). Nothing moves onto it; after every move it drifts a hex outwards, the way the spill spreads (`flotDrift`); off the edge, or when its hexes fall in (a collapse, a new spill), it floats on the lake like a bottle until it is off the screen or sinks (`flotLake`).
- **Crates, Cases and Caches** are boxes: their faces as before, locked to the surface's plane, their depth behind them in the crate's planks (`drawCrateDepth`: `crateDepth`, `crateDepthAng`; a Cache keeps its coloured glass on its face only).
- **Next made** (Build menu): tune what Create makes next within the game's own ranges — mass, energy and exact colour balance of a particle, pigment, Point, remnant or relic, and what a package holds (`NEXT`, `nextMade`).

## The Guide
Every entry is a card in one format (name, a live demo, page and Tier I–V, then its details and live math, each behind its own discovery). Pages: **Discoveries** (how to play, how to clean up, and the events witnessed), **Taxonomy** (every kind of thing and what it becomes, drawn with the game's own glyphs; its cards below), Items, Shapes, Surface Map, **Shops** (How shops work on top: main street, pop-ups, buying; then each shop's card, only its sign's shape until it has opened, with what it does and a tab for each currency it takes), Ducks, Glossary. (Status, your ducks, letters and souvenirs are in **Status & collections**, below.) **Event capture**: the moment an event first happens (where it happened: a pigment, a Point, a remnant, a relic, a duck, a Dark Remnant, a Rift), the game does not stop at once: a faint frame with sparkles follows it, wrapping with it, brighter as it starts, sized by its kind (`EV_FRAME`: small, mid, large, the whole screen for a Rift; `evFrameS/M/L`), the game slows by `evSlow` (with the novas' slows) and the scene is recorded (`evPreMs` before it, `evCaptureMs` of it). If nothing else happens for `evSoloMs` the game pauses and the frame grows into the event's card (`evZoomMs`), its art the recording of your own moment (`evClipPlay`). Another event during one sends the first to the discovery desk (its count by the dock: `evDesk`), and after the last its card shows with the rest in its hand. An event is discovered when it happens (the Guide shows it at once); it stays marked unread (●) until it is looked at, in any place (`evUnread`). A shop's first opening is an event (it is discovered then: the Corner Store and Gift Shop on the first morning, the others when they first open). Discoveries made together come as a hand: one card shown, the others below it to pick; closing it eases time back in slowly (`evResumeMs`).

## Play together
Esc on a surface → **Invite a friend** copies a link (`join.html?room=…`). The friend's page shows the host's surface as a live video (PeerJS / WebRTC, peer to peer; the PeerJS public server only makes the introduction) and sends their pointer. On the host it shines, wipes, mops and collects with the same shine cap (`maxActivePoints`) but its own heat; what the friend collects is added to the friend's own save. Add `&net=bc` to the surface's URL to play between two tabs of one browser (BroadcastChannel, no network).

## Performance
The Surface Map draws its ground on a 2D canvas and sends it to the GPU every frame, so its size is the main cost: `mapPlanePx` caps it, and its sharpness follows the zoom. While the map's window is not the one in use it draws `mapIdleFps` (8) frames a second. A hex's picture is built straight from the surface's particle map (only the cells it shows, its blur passes drawn into the picture, no full frame and no start snapshot): about 12 ms instead of 60, one a frame, so a whole map of new pictures eases in over a second or two. A hex's coverage is its value itself, to 1% (`cellCov`: a value of 0.3 is a surface 30% covered, out to full at `mapCovFull`), so a picture is rebuilt whenever a hex's value moves by 1%; however bare, its particles keep their full mass and energy. The light moves round to a new time of day over `mapSunMs` (4.5 s). The performance log keeps only the last 600 frames, 25 slowest and 400 events, so a map left open does not grow it.

- **Typing a tuner's value**: on a surface, in Build mode and on the Surface Map alike, the value beside each slider is a box: type a number, then Enter (or click away) sets it, corrected into its range and onto its nearest step (`tuneSnap`, `tuneValInput` in `shared.js`); Esc puts it back.

Lake ducks (and bottles): brush one slowly and it spins, hit it fast and it putters off, click it and it is dunked; how fast it spins, how hard it is pushed and how much it turns as it goes all follow how fast the cursor moved. Now and then a message in a bottle floats in (`mapBottleMs`): a 3D clear, green or brown glass bottle (drawn by the duck renderer, `bottleModel` in `shared.js`), half under the water and reflected in it. It drifts like a duck; clicking it opens a letter (`LETTERS`), or, for one bottle in four (`mapBottleDuck`), a duck, which joins your collection (one you did not have bobs up onto the lake). A duck's bottle is gold for an exotic, opaque white for a classic, and shines when the duck is new to you. Bottles float upright, leaning, about half under; an empty one can be dunked like a duck. A brush or a hit counts once, until the cursor has come fully off; a dunk sinks it under the waterline and it bobs back up, and the empty bottle goes back on the water. Letters found are kept (`il_letters_v1`) and read in the **Letters** book (Status & collections) **Double dab** a duck (or an opened bottle) and it goes under, then pops up well out of the water for a moment. **Drag or wipe** over one and it is pushed under and carried with the cursor (the map does not pan meanwhile); let go and it comes up there, on the water or on the spill, but never under a shop (`shopUnder`: its footprint; it slides out from under first). On the spill it wades off, outwards and round any shop or Rift in its way (`duckBlocked`); with no way off for `mapDuckTrapMs` (6 s) it sinks under and is gone.
- **Test mode** (in Modes, on the map and on a surface; `TEST` in `shared.js`, each player's own): turning it on keeps a copy of your save (the surface's purse, discoveries, ducks and run: `il_test_snap_inv`; the map: `il_test_snap_map`), then turns on **Free purchases** (every shop price 0, ducks too, up to each purse's cap; `shopPrice`), discovers and unlocks everything (`discAll`: the interface, object types, events, the Guide and its map; `unlockAll`: every upgrade owned, so its mechanics run, and one of every duck), and fills every item (`testFill`). - **Collapse** (`ringCheck`, `collapseRing`): a ring of hexes all bright, all dark or all Rift falls into the source with everything inside it (the ring first, each ring in faster). What was cleared in it is counted (a Rift takes back one bright, or a dark when no bright is left; a Rift ring counts nothing) and kept with the day (`dayCnt.col`). The rings past it close in (`shrinkRings`): ring n+1 becomes ring 1 and so on, each hex to the same place round the centre (`ringSlot`, `slotHex`); a nearer ring has fewer hexes, so where two land on one the busier is kept (a shop, a clear, a Rift, then any), and the value left (less what fell in) is shared over fewer hexes. - **Rest** — at Midnight you are too tired to move: a Rest button under the time of day ends the day, so a last thing can be done first (a surface, a shop, an item)
- **Surface Map dock**: pointing at an item names it and says what it does; with it in hand, the feed says what it would do on the hex under the cursor and what it costs. Every button (the ▾ too) has its own see-through backing. - **Surface Map**: Modes (Telemetry: each hex's value, coverage and ring on the ground; Stats: fps, frame times and the hex grid; the Performance log) and Tune (every tuner, as on a surface). - **Spindle** (armed on the map): a cleared hex back to a surface, or an open hex below 1.00 wound up to 1.00 and held there until cleared. An unwound surface keeps its clear's look: a bright one starts white (its particles back), a Dark Remnant one dark
